Topic 1

Parliament

Where the UK’s laws are made.

The UK’s laws are made in Parliament in London. The famous building with Big Ben is the Palace of Westminster — that’s Parliament.

Parliament has two parts (called “Houses”):

  • House of Commons — made up of MPs (Members of Parliament). There are 650 of them. Each one is voted in by people in their part of the country (their constituency).
  • House of Lords — around 800 people who weren’t voted in. Some are there because of their job (bishops). Others were appointed for service to the country.

For a new law to happen, it has to be agreed by BOTH Houses, then signed by the King.

Topic 2

The Prime Minister

In charge of the country.

The Prime Minister (PM) is the leader of the UK government. The PM lives at 10 Downing Street in London — a famous black door you’ll have seen on the news.

The PM is the leader of whichever party won the most seats in the last general election. They choose other MPs to help them — a group called the Cabinet. Each Cabinet member runs a different bit of the country: Health, Education, Money, Defence, etc.

If the country doesn’t like what the PM is doing, they can vote them out at the next general election. UK general elections happen at least every 5 years.

Topic 3

The King & Royal Family

Britain’s longest tradition.

Britain has had a king or queen for over 1,000 years. The current monarch is King Charles III. His mother, Queen Elizabeth II, was queen for 70 years — longer than anyone in British history.

Today the King’s job is mostly ceremonial. He doesn’t make the laws. But he:

  • Signs every new law (a tradition called Royal Assent).
  • Meets the Prime Minister every week.
  • Hosts visiting world leaders.
  • Visits hospitals, schools, communities.
  • Represents the UK abroad.

The monarch never tells people who to vote for. They have to stay above politics.

Topic 4

Local Council

Decisions near you.

Lots of decisions don’t need to go all the way to Parliament. Local stuff is decided by your local council.

Things councils look after:

  • Bin collections.
  • Street lights.
  • Local roads (filling in potholes).
  • Schools.
  • Libraries.
  • Local parks.
  • Building permission for new houses.

Councillors are voted in too — like a small Parliament for your area.

Topic 5

Tax

How public stuff gets paid for.

How does the government pay for schools, hospitals, roads, the police, the army? Through tax.

Tax is money that grown-ups have to pay to the government. The most common kinds:

  • Income tax — a slice of the money you earn from your job.
  • VAT — added onto most things you buy in shops (20% extra).
  • Council tax — a yearly amount each household pays to the local council.

Tax can feel annoying when adults have to pay it. But it’s how everyone pays for the things we all use — the NHS, schools, police, parks, refuse collection.
