Topic 1

Active & Passive Voice

Who is doing the action, and who it’s done to.

An active sentence has a subject doing something to an object:

The cat (subject) chased the mouse (object).

A passive sentence flips it around. The object comes first, and we sometimes don’t even say who did it:

The mouse was chased (by the cat).

Use passive when: the action is more important than who did it, or you don’t know who did it, or you want to be polite.

Topic 2

Subjunctive Form

Polite, formal phrasing.

The subjunctive is used for suggestions, wishes, and formal requests. It often uses “were” and “be”:

  • “If I were a rich person, I would…” (not “if I was”)
  • “I suggest that he be on time.” (not “is”)
  • “It is important that she take her medicine.” (not “takes”)

You don’t use this in chatty writing — only in formal pieces.

Topic 3

Colons, Semicolons & Bullets

Powerful punctuation for upper-year writing.

A colon : means “here it comes”. Use it to introduce:

  • A list: You will need: flour, eggs, sugar.
  • An explanation: He loved one thing: football.

A semicolon ; joins two sentences that are closely linked:

The dog barked; the cat scurried away.

Bullet points help organise a list. Each bullet should be punctuated consistently, usually starting with a capital and ending with a full stop, OR starting lowercase with no full stop (but don’t mix in one list).

Topic 4

Formal & Informal (Year 6)

Reading the room.

In Year 6 we really master formal writing. Clues that something is FORMAL:

  • Uses full words (“do not” not “don’t”).
  • Uses the passive voice often.
  • Uses the subjunctive when needed.
  • Uses sophisticated vocabulary: request, require, therefore, however, nonetheless.
  • Avoids chatty phrases and slang.

When to use formal: letters of complaint, job applications, newspaper articles, essays, scientific reports.

Topic 5

Grammar Recap

All the important grammar terms.

Word classes: noun, verb, adjective, adverb, pronoun, preposition, conjunction, determiner.

Sentence types: simple (one clause), compound (two clauses joined by and/but/or), complex (main clause + subordinate clause).

Subordinate clauses start with words like although, because, if, since, unless, while.

Tenses: past, present, future. Also present perfect (has/have + verb): She has eaten.
