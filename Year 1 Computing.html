Topic 1

What is an Algorithm?

A clear list of steps to do something.

The word algorithm sounds fancy, but it just means a list of steps to do something — in the right order. When you brush your teeth, you follow an algorithm: pick up toothbrush, put toothpaste on, brush the top teeth, brush the bottom teeth, rinse, put toothbrush back. Change the order and it gets silly — try brushing before putting the toothpaste on!

Computers need algorithms too. A computer is not actually clever on its own. It only knows how to follow instructions, one at a time. Whenever a computer does something useful, a human has told it exactly what steps to take.

In Year 1 we learn to write simple algorithms as lists of instructions:

  • Stand up
  • Take 3 steps forward
  • Turn right
  • Take 2 steps forward
  • Sit down
Topic 2

Programs

When a computer follows an algorithm.

A program is an algorithm written in a language a computer understands. When we turn an algorithm into a program, we can run it over and over again.

In school you might use special child-friendly software like Scratch Junior or a Bee-Bot — a little robot on wheels. You press buttons to tell the Bee-Bot which way to go. Forward, turn, go again. The list of button presses is your program.

If the Bee-Bot doesn’t end up where you wanted, it’s not its fault — it followed your instructions exactly. You have to spot your mistake and fix it. This is called debugging.

Topic 3

Using Technology

Technology helps us every day.

Technology is all around you. You probably use it every day without even thinking. A tablet, a phone, a TV remote, a washing machine, a toaster, a traffic light, a microwave — all of them use technology.

In Year 1 we use computers and tablets to: type our name, draw pictures, take photos, play educational games, and record our voice. You learn to tap, swipe, and type — small skills that open up a huge world.

Technology has parts you might know: a screen to see, a keyboard to type, a mouse or touchpad to point, speakers to hear, a microphone to record.

Topic 4

Staying Safe Online

Being careful with computers, just like crossing the road.

Computers and tablets are great fun, but just like crossing a road, you need to be careful. Some strangers online might not be nice. Some messages might not be true. Some things you see might upset you.

Three golden rules for Year 1:

  • Tell a grown-up if anything online upsets you or makes you feel funny. Never keep it a secret.
  • Never share your name, address, school or phone number with someone online. These are private things.
  • Only use computers and tablets when a grown-up knows you are on them.
Topic 5

Creating with Computers

Making pictures, sounds and stories.

Computers aren’t just for reading and watching — they’re brilliant for MAKING things. In Year 1 you might:

  • Paint a picture using a drawing app on a tablet.
  • Record yourself telling a story.
  • Take photos of your classroom.
  • Type simple sentences about your family.

Computers let you fix mistakes easily. On paper, one wrong crayon mark can ruin your picture. On a tablet, you press “undo” and your picture is perfect again. That’s a superpower.
