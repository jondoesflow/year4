Topic 1

Drawing with Control

Pencil techniques.

Year 3 drawing focuses on control. Instead of grabbing any old pencil, we think about which grade to use. Pencils are rated from 9H (hardest, lightest mark) to 9B (softest, darkest mark). HB is in the middle. 2B is a nice dark smudgy pencil — try using it for darker areas of your drawing.

Techniques to practise:

  • Hatching — parallel lines to show shadow.
  • Cross-hatching — two sets of parallel lines crossed, for darker shadow.
  • Shading — smooth tone from light to dark.
  • Smudging — use your finger or a tissue to blend.
Topic 2

Painting Techniques

Watercolour and poster paint.

Year 3 painters work with watercolour as well as the poster paint from earlier years. Watercolour is much more delicate. Start with lots of water and less paint for washes (pale, see-through layers). Add less water and more paint for stronger colour.

Layering is key: paint the lightest colours first, let them dry, then add darker colours on top. You can’t easily paint light over dark with watercolour.

Topic 3

Printmaking

Stamps, potatoes, polystyrene.

Printmaking lets you make the same image lots of times. You make a block that you ink up and press onto paper.

Year 3 printmaking methods:

  • Potato printing — cut a shape out of half a potato, ink it, stamp.
  • Polystyrene printing — scratch a design into a flat polystyrene tile, ink it, press onto paper.
  • String printing — glue string in a shape to a board, ink, press.
Topic 4

Sculpture

Clay, wire, papier mâché.

Year 3 sculpture uses more grown-up materials: real clay, wire, and papier mâché. Papier mâché is French for “chewed paper” — you tear up newspaper, dip it in glue and water, and layer it over a shape. When it dries, it sets hard.

Wire sculpture: bend wire into shapes — figures, animals, abstract forms. Wire sculptures by Alexander Calder are famous.

Topic 5

Famous Artists

Three more to meet.

Year 3 artists:

  • Leonardo da Vinci (1452 – 1519) — Italian Renaissance master. Painter of the Mona Lisa and The Last Supper.
  • Claude Monet (1840 – 1926) — French. Leader of Impressionism. Painted water lilies, haystacks, and his garden over and over.
  • Bridget Riley (born 1931) — British. Famous for eye-bending black-and-white pattern paintings called Op Art.
