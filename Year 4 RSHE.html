Topic 1

Mental Health

Looking after your mind.

You know about looking after your body. Looking after your mind is just as important. We call this mental health.

Good mental health doesn’t mean being happy all the time — nobody is. It means feeling safe, being able to handle problems, having people you can talk to, and feeling generally OK.

Things that help mental health:

  • Sleep, exercise, eating well.
  • Time with people who care about you.
  • Time outdoors and in nature.
  • Doing things you enjoy.
  • Talking about your feelings instead of bottling them up.
  • Asking for help when you need it.

If you feel sad or anxious for a long time, that’s not weakness — that’s a sign you need support. Tell a grown-up.

Topic 2

Consent

Asking, listening, respecting.

Consent means saying YES to something. Important consent rules:

  • You always have the right to say NO to a hug, a touch, or a tickle. Even from family.
  • If you say no, others must respect it.
  • If someone says no to you, you must respect it. “But I was only joking” isn’t a good answer.
  • Silence isn’t consent. Looking unsure isn’t consent. Only a clear yes is consent.

This goes for your photos too. If a friend asks you not to post a photo of them, don’t. If you wouldn’t want a photo posted of you, don’t post one of someone else.

Topic 3

Basic First Aid

When someone’s hurt.

If someone’s hurt, calm helps a LOT.

  • Small cut — wash with water, plaster on top.
  • Nosebleed — pinch the soft bit of the nose, lean forward, breathe through your mouth, hold for 10 minutes.
  • Bee sting — if you can see the sting, scrape it out with a card. Cool with a cold cloth.
  • Burn — cool running water for 20 minutes. Don’t put butter or toothpaste on.
  • Fall and bumped head — tell an adult straight away.
  • Someone unconscious — ring 999 immediately and ask for ambulance. Stay with the person.
Topic 4

Drugs & Alcohol

What they are.

Some substances change how your body works. Year 4 covers the basics:

  • Caffeine (in tea, coffee, fizzy drinks) wakes you up. Too much makes you jittery.
  • Alcohol is in beer, wine, spirits. It’s a drug that adults are allowed (but not children). It slows the brain. Too much makes you ill or do dangerous things.
  • Tobacco (in cigarettes, vapes) is highly addictive. It causes cancer and lung disease. It’s ILLEGAL to sell to under-18s.
  • Illegal drugs are even more dangerous because they’re not checked, not measured, and they break the law.
  • Prescribed medicines (from a doctor) help you. They’re only safe if you take the right amount and only YOUR own.

If anyone offers you something you don’t recognise, say no and tell an adult.

Topic 5

Screen Time

Helpful and harmful.

Screens (TV, tablets, phones, consoles) are everywhere. They’re great for learning, talking, and entertainment. But too much screen time isn’t good for you.

Signs you might be having too much:

  • Sore eyes, headaches.
  • Poor sleep.
  • Grumpy when asked to come off.
  • Less time outdoors, less time with friends in person.

Healthy habits:

  • Have screen-free meals.
  • Stop screens 1 hour before bed.
  • For every 30 mins on a screen, take a 5-minute break (stretch, look out the window).
  • Only use age-appropriate apps and games.
