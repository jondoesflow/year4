Topic 1

Designing for a Purpose

Who is it for, and what does it need to do?

Design and Technology (or D&T) is where we design and make things to solve problems. In Year 1 we always ask: who is this for, and what do they need?

Example: design a greetings card. Who is it for (Gran? teacher?). What does it need to do (say Happy Birthday? cheer someone up?). Once you know that, you can choose colours, words, pictures.

Real designers sketch their ideas before making anything. In Year 1 we do simple drawings of what we’re going to make.

Topic 2

Making

Using simple tools safely.

In Year 1 we learn to use simple tools:

  • Scissors — carry them with the pointy end DOWN. Pass them to someone handle-first.
  • Glue sticks — put the lid back on when done, or they dry out.
  • PVA glue — a white glue that dries clear. Use a brush.
  • Tape — masking tape, sticky tape.
  • Hole punch — makes a small round hole.

Safety matters. Sharp tools are for making, not waving about.

Topic 3

Simple Mechanisms

Wheels, hinges, sliders.

A mechanism is a part that moves. Year 1 explores three simple ones:

  • Wheels — go round. Cars have four.
  • Hinges — let things swing open. Doors have hinges. So do books.
  • Sliders — slide in and out. Drawer in a cabinet.

You can make a moving greetings card using a slider — push it in and out to make the sun rise.

Topic 4

Food

Healthy eating and where food comes from.

Good food keeps you healthy. The Eatwell Guide shows how much of each food group you should eat:

  • Fruit and vegetables — aim for 5 a day.
  • Starchy foods (bread, pasta, potatoes) — plenty.
  • Protein (meat, fish, eggs, beans) — some each day.
  • Dairy (milk, cheese, yoghurt) — some each day.
  • Sugary/fatty foods — just a little.

Food comes from somewhere: carrots from the soil, eggs from chickens, cheese from cows, bananas from trees in hot countries. In Year 1 we learn where our food starts.

Topic 5

Evaluating

Looking at what you made and thinking.

After you’ve made something, you evaluate. This means looking at it carefully and thinking:

  • Did it work?
  • Does it do what it was meant to do?
  • What would I change next time?

Evaluation isn’t about being bad at making — it’s how you get better.
