Topic 1

Sounds & Tricky Words

Most words follow the sounds we learned in phonics. A few don’t — those are tricky words.

In Year 2 you meet lots of new spelling sounds:

  • dge at the end of short words: badge, edge, bridge.
  • y as /ee/ at the end of words: happy, pretty, sunny.
  • le at the end: little, middle, bottle.
  • wh for /w/ at the start: when, where, what, who.

Tricky words don’t sound like they look. You have to remember them:

Topic 2

Capital Letters & Full Stops

Every sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with a full stop.

Start every sentence with a CAPITAL LETTER. End it with a full stop (.), a question mark (?), or an exclamation mark (!).

  • Use a full stop when you finish telling something: The cat is black.
  • Use a question mark when you ask: Are you hungry?
  • Use an exclamation mark when you shout or are excited: Look out!

Capital letters also go at the start of:

  • Names of people: Emma, Ravi, Jamila.
  • Names of places: London, France, the River Thames.
  • Days: Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday.
  • Months: January, February, March.
  • The word I (always a capital, even in the middle of a sentence).
Spot the difference

❌ my name is tom.

✓ My name is Tom.

Capital for the start of the sentence. Capital for Tom (a name).

Topic 3

Building Sentences

Joining words glue short sentences into bigger ones.

A sentence tells us one idea. Two small sentences can join into one using a joining word:

  • and — for adding: I had a snack and I drank some juice.
  • but — for something different: I was tired but I kept going.
  • or — for a choice: Would you like an apple or a pear?
  • because — for giving a reason: I am happy because it is sunny.

Remember: every new sentence gets a capital letter at the start, even if it follows a joining word with a new idea.

Topic 4

Nouns, Verbs & Adjectives

Every word has a job.

A noun is a naming word — a person, place, animal, or thing:

  • Emma, teacher, shop, cat, book.

A verb is a doing word. It says what somebody or something IS doing:

  • run, eat, sleep, jump, laugh.

An adjective is a describing word. It tells us more about a noun:

  • a red apple, a kind teacher, a loud noise, a sunny day.
Spot them in a sentence

The fluffy dog barked.

Adjective = fluffy. Noun = dog. Verb = barked.

Topic 5

Adding Endings

Adding little endings changes what a word means.

-ing and -ed turn a doing word into something happening now or in the past:

  • jump → jumping, jumped
  • play → playing, played
  • walk → walking, walked

-er and -est compare things:

  • big → bigger → biggest
  • fast → faster → fastest
  • kind → kinder → kindest

-ly changes a describing word into a “how” word:

  • quick → quickly
  • kind → kindly
  • slow → slowly
Top spelling tip

For words ending in y like "happy", change the y to i before adding -er or -est.

happy → happier → happiest

Topic 6

Reading Stories

Reading is a window into other worlds.

Good readers do three things:

  • Sound it out. Use your phonics to read the letters in tricky words.
  • Picture it. As you read, make a little film in your head. What does the room look like? How does the character feel?
  • Talk about it. Who are the characters? What happens at the start, middle, and end?

A story usually has:

  • A beginning — who the characters are and where they live.
  • A middle — something happens! A problem, a surprise, a challenge.
  • An end — the problem gets solved, and we find out how everyone feels.

If you find a word you don’t know, try this: look at the pictures, say it out loud, read the sentence again, or ask a grown-up.
