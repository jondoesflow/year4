Topic 1

Ancient Greece

The beginning of democracy, philosophy and the Olympics.

Ancient Greece was a set of city-states (each its own little country) in what is now Greece, from about 800 BC to 150 BC. The most famous were Athens and Sparta.

Greek gifts to the world:

  • Democracy — ordinary people voting on decisions (Athens).
  • Philosophy — thinkers like Socrates, Plato, Aristotle.
  • Theatre — tragedy, comedy, masked actors.
  • The Olympic Games — started 776 BC.
  • Myths and legends — Zeus, Athena, Hercules, the Trojan War.
Topic 2

Anglo-Saxons & Scots

After the Romans left.

When the Romans left Britain around 410 AD, new peoples arrived:

  • Angles, Saxons and Jutes sailed from Germany, Denmark and the Netherlands. They became the Anglo-Saxons.
  • Scots came from Ireland and settled in what became Scotland.

Anglo-Saxons divided Britain into seven kingdoms (the Heptarchy): Wessex, Mercia, Northumbria, Kent, Sussex, Essex, East Anglia.

They spoke Old English. They became Christian after St Augustine arrived from Rome in 597 AD.

Topic 3

Vikings (in more detail)

Raiders, traders, and settlers.

From 793 AD, Vikings from Norway, Denmark and Sweden raided, traded and settled across the British Isles.

They:

  • Sacked the monastery at Lindisfarne in 793.
  • Settled the Danelaw — a huge area of northern and eastern England under Viking law.
  • Made Jorvik (York) their capital in Britain.
  • Reached Iceland, Greenland and North America 500 years before Columbus.

King Alfred the Great of Wessex defeated the Vikings in 878 AD. His grandson Athelstan became the first king of all of England in 927.

Topic 4

Early Islamic Baghdad

The most learned city on Earth, 1,200 years ago.

In 762 AD, a new city was founded in what is now Iraq: Baghdad. For about 500 years it was the centre of learning, science, and trade in the world.

Things that happened in early Islamic Baghdad:

  • A vast library called the House of Wisdom, where scholars translated Greek, Persian and Indian books.
  • Huge advances in maths (the word “algebra” comes from an Arabic book).
  • Important work in astronomy, medicine and geography.
  • The invention of our number system (0 – 9, called Arabic numerals).
Topic 5

Putting it in Order

Britain from Romans to Normans.

  • 43 AD — Romans invade Britain.
  • ~410 AD — Romans leave Britain.
  • ~450 AD — Anglo-Saxons begin to settle.
  • 597 AD — St Augustine brings Christianity.
  • 793 AD — Vikings raid Lindisfarne.
  • 878 AD — Alfred defeats the Vikings.
  • 927 AD — Athelstan, first King of all England.
  • 1066 AD — Battle of Hastings. Normans invade.
