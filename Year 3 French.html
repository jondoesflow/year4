Topic 1

Greetings & Introductions

Saying hello in French.

French is spoken by over 300 million people across France, Belgium, Switzerland, Canada, and lots of African countries. When you learn French you can talk to all of them.

Here are the most important words:

  • Bonjour (say: bon-zhoor) — Hello / Good morning
  • Salut (sa-loo) — Hi (informal)
  • Au revoir (oh ruh-vwar) — Goodbye
  • S’il vous plaît (seel voo pleh) — Please
  • Merci (mair-see) — Thank you
  • Oui (wee) — Yes   Non (noh) — No

To ask someone their name: Comment tu t’appelles? (How are you called?). To answer: Je m’appelle + your name (I am called…).

Topic 2

Numbers 1 to 20

Counting in French.

Numbers are used all day — age, time, money, counting. Learn them early and everything else in French becomes easier.

  • 1 un (uhn)
  • 2 deux (duh)
  • 3 trois (twah)
  • 4 quatre (katr)
  • 5 cinq (sank)
  • 6 six (seess)
  • 7 sept (set)
  • 8 huit (weet)
  • 9 neuf (nuhf)
  • 10 dix (deess)

Eleven to twenty are their own words (unlike English “thirteen, fourteen”): onze, douze, treize, quatorze, quinze, seize, then dix-sept (ten-seven), dix-huit, dix-neuf, vingt.

To ask how old: Quel âge as-tu? Answer: J’ai huit ans (I have eight years — French uses HAVE not AM for age).

Topic 3

Colours

Les couleurs.

Colours in French:

  • rouge (roozh) — red
  • bleu (bluh) — blue
  • jaune (zhohn) — yellow
  • vert (vair) — green
  • noir (nwar) — black
  • blanc (blonk) — white
  • rose (rohz) — pink
  • orange (or-onzh) — orange

In French, colour words come AFTER the thing they describe: un chat noir (a cat black). This is the opposite of English.

Topic 4

Family

Ma famille.

Family words:

  • mon père — my dad
  • ma mère — my mum
  • mon frère — my brother
  • ma sœur — my sister
  • mon grand-père — my grandad
  • ma grand-mère — my grandma

French has TWO words for “my”: mon (for masculine words) and ma (for feminine words). Every noun in French is either masculine or feminine — you have to learn which is which.

Topic 5

In the Classroom

La salle de classe.

Things you find in your salle de classe:

  • un livre — a book
  • un crayon — a pencil
  • un stylo — a pen
  • une règle — a ruler
  • une trousse — a pencil case
  • un cahier — an exercise book

Notice un for masculine and une for feminine — French’s way of saying “a”.
