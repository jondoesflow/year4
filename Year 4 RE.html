Topic 1

Sikhism

A religion born in Punjab.

Sikhism began about 500 years ago in a region called Punjab, in north India. The founder was Guru Nanak.

Sikhs believe in ONE God. They believe all humans are equal — no matter their religion, race, or job. That idea was very radical in India 500 years ago.

Sikhs worship in a gurdwara. The most famous is the Golden Temple at Amritsar. Every gurdwara has a kitchen called the langar that serves free meals to anyone who walks in — sitting on the floor together, rich and poor side by side.

Observant Sikhs wear the Five Ks — uncut hair (covered by a turban), a comb, a small sword, a steel bracelet, and special undergarments. Each is a reminder of their faith.

Topic 2

Buddhism

The Buddha and his teaching.

Buddhism began in India about 2,500 years ago when a prince called Siddhartha gave up his riches and searched for the meaning of life. After sitting under a tree for 49 days he became the Buddha — “the awakened one.”

Buddhists don’t usually believe in a god. Instead they focus on:

  • Living kindly.
  • Not being greedy.
  • Meditating (sitting quietly to calm the mind).
  • Following the Eightfold Path (eight guides for good living).

Buddhism spread all across Asia. Today there are millions of Buddhists in countries like Thailand, Japan, China, and Sri Lanka. Buddhist temples often have huge statues of the Buddha, smell of incense, and echo with the sound of bells.

Topic 3

Passover

A Jewish festival about freedom.

The most important Jewish festival is Passover (Pesach). It remembers a story from over 3,000 years ago.

The Jewish people were slaves in Egypt. A leader called Moses stood up to the Egyptian king (pharaoh) and said “Let my people go!” God sent ten plagues on Egypt. Finally pharaoh agreed. The Jewish people escaped — crossing the Red Sea as it parted in front of them.

Today Jewish families eat a special meal called a Seder. On the plate:

  • Matzah — flat bread (they had no time for bread to rise).
  • Bitter herbs — to remind them of slavery.
  • Salt water — to remind them of tears.
  • Haroset — a sweet mix, reminding them of the mortar they worked with.
Topic 4

Hajj

The Muslim pilgrimage.

Once in their life, if they can afford it, every Muslim tries to make a pilgrimage called Hajj to the city of Mecca in Saudi Arabia.

Over 2 million people from every country in the world arrive in Mecca in the same week. All wear simple white cloth, so rich and poor look the same. They walk seven times around a black cube called the Ka’ba — the most sacred place in Islam.

Hajj is one of the Five Pillars of Islam. The other four are:

  • Shahada — declaring belief in one God and the prophet Muhammad.
  • Salah — praying five times a day.
  • Zakat — giving money to poor people.
  • Sawm — fasting during Ramadan.
Topic 5

Big Questions

Why are we here?

Religions try to answer BIG questions that science can’t answer:

  • Why is there something rather than nothing?
  • What happens when we die?
  • Why is there suffering?
  • How should we live?

Different religions give different answers. Some people have no religion and look for their own answers. It’s important to listen, think, and respect that people will answer differently — and they can still be friends.
