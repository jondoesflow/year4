Topic 1

What happens after we die?

One of the hardest questions.

In Year 6 RE we look at maybe the hardest question of all: what happens after death?

Christians believe in heaven, a place of perfect happiness with God, for those who followed Jesus. Some also believe in hell for those who didn’t.

Muslims similarly believe in Jannah (paradise) and Jahannam (hell). On the Day of Judgement, every person’s actions are weighed.

Hindus and Buddhists believe in reincarnation — when you die, your soul is born again in a new body. What kind of body depends on your karma. The ultimate goal is to break free from this cycle (moksha in Hinduism, nirvana in Buddhism).

Many people today — including many scientists — believe nothing happens after death: you simply end, like a candle going out. These are hugely different answers. We think about each one.

Topic 3

Tough Choices

Ethics & religion.

Religions don’t just tell stories — they help people decide what’s RIGHT when it’s HARD. Some tough questions Year 6 thinks about:

  • Is it ever OK to kill? (Most religions say no, but many allow self-defence or protecting others.)
  • Is it OK to eat animals? (Hindus and Buddhists often say no. Jews and Muslims have rules: kosher / halal.)
  • Should we share our money with poor people? (Every religion says yes, strongly.)
  • Is it wrong to tell a small lie to protect someone? (Religions differ.)

Thinking about these carefully — even disagreeing — is part of growing up. You might not land where your family lands. That’s OK.

Topic 4

Science and Religion

Do they agree?

Lots of people think science and religion argue. It’s more complicated than that.

Some of the greatest scientists in history were deeply religious:

  • Isaac Newton (gravity) — wrote more about the Bible than about physics.
  • Gregor Mendel (genetics) — was a Christian monk.
  • Georges Lemaître (Big Bang theory) — was a Catholic priest.

Many scientists today have no religion. They think science fully explains the world and nothing more is needed.

Many religious people accept the Big Bang and evolution, and say it’s just HOW God made the universe — the science is the “what,” the religion is the “why.”

Both views are held by thoughtful, clever people. It’s a brilliant conversation to join.

Topic 5

People with No Religion

A growing group.

In the UK today, more than half of people say they don’t follow any religion. Different labels describe this:

  • Atheist — thinks there is no God.
  • Agnostic — says we can’t know if there’s a God.
  • Humanist — focuses on human kindness and reason without God.
  • Secular — keeps religion out of public life (e.g. government).

People with no religion still face the big questions. They look for meaning through family, friendship, nature, art, science, helping others, and trying to live a good life. Many of the kindest people in the world are religious. Many of the kindest are not.
