Topic 1

UK Mountains & Rivers

The shape of our country.

The UK has mountains, especially in Scotland and Wales:

  • Ben Nevis (Scotland) — tallest in the UK, 1,345 m.
  • Snowdon (Wales) — tallest in Wales.
  • Scafell Pike (England) — tallest in England.

Big rivers:

  • Severn — longest in UK, 220 miles.
  • Thames — flows through London.
  • Trent — in the Midlands.
Topic 2

Countries of Europe

Our big continent.

Europe has around 44 countries. Capitals to know:

  • France — Paris
  • Spain — Madrid
  • Italy — Rome
  • Germany — Berlin
  • Portugal — Lisbon
  • Netherlands — Amsterdam
  • Ireland — Dublin
  • Greece — Athens

The UK is part of Europe, on the north-west edge.

Topic 3

Settlements

Where people choose to live.

People choose where to live based on what they need. Early settlers looked for:

  • Water — a river or spring to drink and wash.
  • Flat land — for building and farming.
  • Shelter — from wind, cold and enemies.
  • Food sources — good soil, animals, fish.
  • Good roads or routes — to trade.

Settlements come in sizes: hamlet (few houses) → village → town → city.

Topic 4

The Water Cycle

Water goes round and round the Earth.

The water on Earth is always moving. The steps repeat forever:

  1. Evaporation — the Sun heats the sea and lakes. Water turns into invisible vapour and rises.
  2. Condensation — high in the sky, the vapour cools and turns back into tiny water drops. These make clouds.
  3. Precipitation — when drops get heavy enough, they fall as rain, snow or hail.
  4. Collection — water flows down into rivers, lakes and back to the sea.

Then it all happens again.

Topic 5

How We Use the Land

Every piece of land does a job.

Land in the UK is used for different things:

  • Residential — houses and flats.
  • Commercial — shops, offices, restaurants.
  • Industrial — factories, warehouses.
  • Agricultural — farms and fields.
  • Leisure — parks, sports fields.
  • Transport — roads, railways, airports.
