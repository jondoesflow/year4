Topic 1

The United Kingdom

The UK is four countries in one.

The four countries of the UK are:

  • England — capital city London.
  • Scotland — capital city Edinburgh.
  • Wales — capital city Cardiff.
  • Northern Ireland — capital city Belfast.

A capital city is the most important city of a country. It’s usually where the government meets.

The land called the British Isles is made up of two big islands: Great Britain (England + Scotland + Wales) and Ireland (Northern Ireland + the Republic of Ireland).

All around the UK is the sea. There are also lots of small islands like the Isle of Wight, Skye, and the Isles of Scilly.

Flags

The Union Jack is the UK’s flag. It mixes three flags together:

  • England’s red cross of St George.
  • Scotland’s diagonal white cross of St Andrew.
  • Northern Ireland’s red diagonal cross of St Patrick.

Wales has its own flag with a red dragon!

Topic 2

Continents & Oceans

The world is split into 7 continents and 5 oceans.

A continent is a huge piece of land. The seven continents are:

  • Africa — home to the Sahara Desert and lions, elephants, zebras.
  • Antarctica — the icy continent at the bottom. Penguins live here.
  • Asia — the biggest continent. Includes China, India, Japan.
  • Australia / Oceania — the smallest continent. Kangaroos!
  • Europe — where the UK is. Also France, Spain, Germany.
  • North America — USA, Canada, Mexico.
  • South America — Brazil, Argentina. Home to the Amazon rainforest.

The five oceans are:

  • Pacific Ocean — the biggest, on the west of the Americas.
  • Atlantic Ocean — between the Americas and Europe/Africa.
  • Indian Ocean — below India.
  • Arctic Ocean — at the very top of the world.
  • Southern Ocean — around Antarctica at the bottom.
Remember them

A silly sentence helps you remember the continents:

"Aunties And Always Are Eating Nice Sweets."

Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Australia, Europe, North America, South America.

Topic 3

Hot & Cold Places

The Equator is the hottest place. The poles are the coldest.

Imagine a belt around the middle of the Earth. That’s the Equator. The Sun shines most strongly there, so it’s very hot all year. Countries near the Equator include Brazil, Kenya, Indonesia, and the northern bits of Australia.

At the very top and very bottom of the Earth are the North Pole and the South Pole. The Sun’s rays hit these places at a low angle, so it’s very cold. There is ice and snow all year.

The UK is in between. It’s not super hot and not super cold. That’s why we have four seasons — spring, summer, autumn, winter.

Animals are suited to their places:

  • Hot places — camels, lions, lizards, snakes.
  • Cold places — polar bears, penguins, seals, arctic foxes.
Topic 4

Weather

The UK has LOTS of different weather — sometimes all in one day!

The main kinds of weather we talk about:

  • Sunny — the Sun is bright and warm.
  • Cloudy — the sky is grey, but no rain.
  • Rainy — water falls from the clouds.
  • Snowy — frozen water drops, white and fluffy.
  • Windy — the air is moving fast.
  • Foggy — a thick, damp cloud on the ground.
  • Stormy — heavy rain with thunder and lightning.

A weather forecast tells you what the weather will probably be tomorrow. The person who reads it on TV is called a weather presenter. Symbols on the map mean:

  • A sun → sunny
  • A cloud → cloudy
  • A cloud with drops → rain
  • A cloud with flakes → snow
Topic 5

Maps & Compasses

A map is a picture of a place, seen from above.

A map is a picture from above — like a bird’s-eye view. Maps have symbols (little pictures) to mean different things:

  • A tree = a wood or park.
  • A cross = a church.
  • Blue squiggles = a river.
  • A little aeroplane = an airport.

The key tells you what the symbols mean.

Every map has compass points:

  • North (N) — up
  • East (E) — right
  • South (S) — down
  • West (W) — left

Remember the order with: "Never Eat Shredded Wheat" (going clockwise from the top).
