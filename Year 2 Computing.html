Topic 1

Algorithms & Sequences

Order matters.

In Year 2 we build on what we learned in Year 1. An algorithm is still a list of steps, but now we look carefully at the order. Changing the order changes what happens — sometimes in a funny way.

Try this: step 1 — put your shoes on. Step 2 — put your socks on. That’s silly! Your socks won’t fit over your shoes. The order matters.

A sequence is an ordered list. Every story has a sequence (beginning, middle, end). Every recipe has a sequence. Every dance has a sequence. Programs are just like those — one step at a time, in the correct order.

Topic 2

Programs & Debugging

Making things happen on screen.

Year 2 takes us further into programming. We can use ScratchJr on a tablet. ScratchJr lets you drag colourful blocks that tell a character what to do: move right, jump up, say hello, play a sound.

When your program doesn’t do what you expected, it has a bug. A bug is a mistake. Finding and fixing bugs is called debugging. Good coders spend lots of time debugging — it’s just part of the job.

Three tips for debugging:

  • Run the program and WATCH what happens.
  • Look at each step — which one went wrong?
  • Change it and try again.
Topic 3

Predicting What Will Happen

Reading the program before you run it.

A good programmer can look at an algorithm and predict what will happen before running it. This is called logical reasoning. It’s thinking clearly one step at a time.

Example: Bee-Bot is at the start. The instructions are: forward, forward, right, forward. Can you trace where Bee-Bot ends up? Close your eyes and picture it. That’s logical reasoning.

Being able to predict saves time. If you can tell the program has a bug just by reading it, you don’t need to run it at all.

Topic 4

Technology Beyond School

Where we use computers in the world.

Computers aren’t just in schools. Almost everywhere you go, computers are quietly working in the background. In Year 2 we look for technology in the real world:

  • Supermarkets — the till scans barcodes, the card machine takes payments, the freezer has a temperature sensor.
  • Cars — modern cars have dozens of computers. One works the engine, another the airbags, another the sat-nav.
  • Hospitals — machines monitor patients, X-rays are digital, prescriptions are printed.
  • Homes — smart speakers, TVs, heating, washing machines.
Topic 5

Being Safe Online

Sensible rules for the internet.

Year 2 goes deeper on online safety. Remember these rules:

  • Keep personal information private. Your full name, home address, school, phone number, birthday — none of these should go to strangers online.
  • Kind words only. Typing something mean still counts as being unkind. Don’t write anything you wouldn’t say to someone’s face.
  • Tell a grown-up. If anything worries you online, tell a trusted adult straight away. You’re not in trouble for telling.
  • Not everything online is true. Some websites and videos make things up. Ask a grown-up if you’re not sure.
