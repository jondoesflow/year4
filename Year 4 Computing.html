Topic 1

Selection (If…Then)

Making programs think and choose.

So far, our programs have done the same thing no matter what. In Year 4 we learn selection — making the computer choose what to do next based on something. We use an if…then statement:

IF the score is 10 THEN play a cheer sound

In Scratch, the if-then block has a diamond-shaped hole where you drop in a question (like “is the score 10?”) and a space inside where you put the instructions to run when the answer is YES.

Selection makes games much more interesting. If the player touches the green flag, win. If they touch the bat, lose. Without if-then, every game would play exactly the same every time.

Topic 2

Variables

Places to store information.

A variable is a named container that holds a value. The value can change as the program runs. In a game, you might have a variable called score that starts at 0 and goes up every time you catch something.

To use variables you need to:

  • Create the variable and give it a name.
  • Set its starting value.
  • Change it during the program (add to it, subtract from it).
  • Use it somewhere (show it on screen, compare it in an if statement).

Variables can hold numbers (score: 15) or text (player-name: “Esme”). They’re everywhere in code.

Topic 3

How the Internet Works

Packets, servers and browsers.

When you type a web address into a browser (like Chrome or Safari) and press Enter, a lot happens in half a second:

  1. Your browser asks a special service (called a DNS) to look up the address.
  2. It sends a request across the internet to a computer called a server.
  3. The server fires back the web page, broken into tiny packages called packets.
  4. The packets arrive at your computer and the browser puts them back together into the page you see.

The internet isn’t one single wire — it’s a huge web of cables, fibre, satellites and radio links. Your message could take hundreds of different routes and still arrive safely.

Topic 4

Data & Spreadsheets

Organising information with numbers and words.

Data is a fancy word for information. Numbers, words, photos, sounds — all data. Computers are brilliant at storing and sorting huge amounts of data.

A spreadsheet is a grid of cells. Each cell holds one piece of data. Cells are arranged in columns (named with letters: A, B, C) and rows (numbered: 1, 2, 3). Cell B3 is the cell in column B, row 3.

Spreadsheets can do clever maths automatically. Type =A1+A2 in a cell, and the spreadsheet works out the answer. Change A1 or A2, and the sum updates instantly. This makes spreadsheets brilliant for budgets, tables, and charts.

Topic 5

Being a Good Digital Citizen

Your behaviour online counts.

As you spend more time online, you become a digital citizen. Being a good one matters. Rules for Year 4:

  • Check your sources. Is this website trustworthy? Is it trying to sell you something?
  • Copyright matters. You can’t just copy a photo or a song off the internet. Someone made it. Look for ones labelled “Creative Commons” which are free to use.
  • Digital footprint. Everything you post online stays there, maybe forever. Would you be happy for your granny to see this?
  • Fake news is real. Many online things are made up to trick people. A shocking headline isn’t proof of anything.
