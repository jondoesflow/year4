Topic 1

Bigger Numbers & BIDMAS

Up to 10 million, plus the rules for the order of operations.

In Year 6 we work with numbers up to 10 million. We also use negative numbers in real contexts, like temperatures and bank balances.

BIDMAS tells us the order to do maths:

  • Brackets
  • Indices (powers like 2³)
  • Divide
  • Multiply
  • Add
  • Subtract

Example: 3 + 4 × 2. Multiply first: 4 × 2 = 8. Then add: 3 + 8 = 11. (Not 14!)

Topic 2

Long Multiplication & Division

The grown-up methods for tricky numbers.

Long multiplication: multiply each digit of the bottom number by each digit of the top number, carefully setting out each row. Example: 246 × 37.

Long division (sometimes called the “bus-stop method”): work from left to right, divide each digit, carry the remainder. Example: 864 ÷ 6 = 144.

Always estimate first: 864 ÷ 6 is roughly 900 ÷ 6 = 150. So your answer should be near 150.

Topic 3

Algebra

Using letters to stand for numbers.

Algebra is maths where letters stand in for unknown numbers.

If x + 5 = 12, then x = 7. We can write this as: to find x, take 5 away from both sides.

Year 6 algebra:

  • Write simple formulas: perimeter of a square = 4s (where s is the side length).
  • Solve simple equations: 2n = 10 means n = 5.
  • Find number sequences and describe the rule.
Topic 4

Fractions, Decimals, Percentages, Ratio

All the ways to share amounts.

Year 6 uses fractions with all four operations:

  • Add / subtract fractions with different denominators (first make them match).
  • Multiply fractions: 1/2 × 1/3 = 1/6 (multiply tops, multiply bottoms).
  • Divide fractions: flip the second fraction, then multiply.

Percentage of a number: 20% of 150 = 0.20 × 150 = 30.

Ratio: a way to compare amounts. 2 : 3 means “2 parts to 3 parts”. If there are 10 sweets in a 2:3 ratio, that’s 4 and 6.

Topic 5

Geometry & Coordinates

Shapes, angles and pinpointing locations.

Year 6 geometry:

  • Angles on a straight line = 180°. Around a point = 360°.
  • Angles in a triangle always add to 180°.
  • Angles in a quadrilateral always add to 360°.
  • Coordinates in four quadrants: using negative numbers on the x-axis (left) and y-axis (down). Write as (x, y).
Topic 6

Statistics

Pie charts, line graphs and the mean average.

Year 6 reads and draws:

  • Line graphs — great for showing change over time.
  • Pie charts — circles split into slices. The whole circle is 100% or 360°.
  • Bar charts, tables, timetables.

The mean average: add all the numbers, then divide by how many there are.

Example: the mean of 2, 4, 6, 8 = (2+4+6+8) ÷ 4 = 20 ÷ 4 = 5.
