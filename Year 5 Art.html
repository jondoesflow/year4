Topic 1

Portrait Drawing

Drawing faces.

Drawing faces feels impossible until you know the proportions. A face is roughly an oval. The eyes sit HALFWAY down — not near the top as most kids draw them. The nose is about halfway between eyes and chin. The mouth is roughly between nose and chin.

Eyes are about one eye-width apart. Ears sit between the level of the eyes and the bottom of the nose. Every face is a little different, but the basic layout is the same.

Draw what you SEE, not what you THINK a face looks like. Your brain lies. Your eyes tell the truth.

Topic 2

Digital Art

Making art on a screen.

Digital art is art made on a computer or tablet. Apps let you draw, paint, and edit with tools that imitate real brushes, pencils and pens.

Advantages of digital:

  • Unlimited undos.
  • Infinite colours.
  • Easy to share online.
  • Layers — draw the background first, then the character on top, without ruining either.

Loads of professional illustrators and designers work entirely digitally now. The books and cartoons you love were probably made like this.

Topic 3

Architecture & Design

Buildings that art made beautiful.

Architecture is the art of designing buildings. It has to be beautiful AND useful AND safe AND affordable — which makes it one of the toughest art forms.

Famous buildings to know:

  • The Taj Mahal in India — a white marble tomb built in the 1600s. Breathtaking symmetry.
  • The Sydney Opera House in Australia — the white sail-like roofs are one of the most recognisable buildings on Earth.
  • The Colosseum in Rome — an enormous ancient stadium, 2,000 years old.
Topic 4

Using a Sketchbook

The artist’s workbench.

By Year 5, your sketchbook becomes serious. Every artist keeps one. Use yours to:

  • Observe — quick drawings of things you see.
  • Experiment — try a new technique before doing a big piece.
  • Collect — glue in pictures, photos, scraps that inspire you.
  • Plan — sketch ideas for bigger artworks.
  • Annotate — jot notes: what worked, what didn’t.
Topic 5

Famous Artists

Deeper into art history.

Year 5 artists:

  • Michelangelo (1475 – 1564) — Italian Renaissance master. Painted the Sistine Chapel ceiling (lying on his back for 4 years). Sculpted the David.
  • Katsushika Hokusai (1760 – 1849) — Japanese. Famous for The Great Wave off Kanagawa — the most recognised piece of Japanese art in the world.
  • Henry Moore (1898 – 1986) — British sculptor. Giant reclining bronze figures.
