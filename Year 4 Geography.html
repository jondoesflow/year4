Rivers

Rivers shape the land.

A river is a long stream of fresh water that flows over land. Rivers start high up and travel all the way to the sea. They carve valleys, feed fields, and give homes to fish, birds, and people. Let’s follow a river from its birthplace to its end.

Lesson 01

What is a river?

By the end you will…

  • Know what a river is.
  • Understand where a river begins.
  • Name the three stages of a river.

A river is fresh water that flows across the land in a long channel. Most rivers begin high up in hills or mountains. The place where a river starts is called the source. The place where it ends — usually at the sea or a lake — is called the mouth.

Every river has a journey. Scientists split this journey into three parts, called the upper course, the middle course, and the lower course. Each part looks and behaves differently.

Stage 1

Upper Course

Fast and narrow. Cuts a V-shaped valley high in the hills.

Stage 2

Middle Course

Wider and slower. Bends in big curves called meanders.

Stage 3

Lower Course

Wide and slow. Flows into the sea, lake, or another river.

!

Did you know?

The longest river in the UK is the River Severn. It flows for about 220 miles — further than the distance from London to Manchester.

Lesson 02

Features of the Upper Course

By the end you will…

  • Know the water is fast at the start.
  • Understand how V-shaped valleys form.
  • Know the riverbed is made of rocks.

High in the hills, a river has just been born. The water is cold, fast, and full of energy. As it rushes downhill, it carries small rocks and pebbles with it. This wearing-away of the land is called erosion.

Over thousands of years, the fast water cuts a deep groove into the rock. The sides of the groove are steep. This shape, like the letter V, is called a V-shaped valley. When a hard layer of rock sits above a soft layer, the river drops suddenly and makes a waterfall.

1

Waterfall

Where the river drops over a cliff of hard rock.

2

Rapids

Fast, bumpy water tumbling over rocks.

3

Gorge

A deep, narrow valley with steep sides.

Lesson 03

Features of the Middle Course

By the end you will…

  • Know the river is flatter and wider.
  • Understand why rivers meander.
  • Know the riverbed is sand and mud.

After the hills, the land flattens out. The river is no longer in a rush. It grows wider and deeper. Because the water is slower, it starts to drop — or deposit — the sand and mud it was carrying from the hills. This new ground builds up on the inside of bends.

The river begins to curve from side to side in big loops. These loops are called meanders. On the outside of each bend, the water still pushes hard and wears the bank away. On the inside, it drops sand. Slowly, over many years, meanders wriggle and move across the landscape.

Lesson 04

Features of the Lower Course

By the end you will…

  • Know the river is flat and slow.
  • Know the riverbed is mostly mud.
  • Understand what a floodplain is.

Near the end of its journey, the river is wide, slow, and full of silt. It is close to the sea. The land on either side is very flat. When heavy rain falls or snow melts, the river sometimes bursts its banks and spreads out across this flat land. This area is called a floodplain.

Floodplains are some of the best farmland in the world. The mud and sand left behind after a flood — called sediment — makes the soil very rich. Ancient Egyptians built their whole civilisation along the floodplain of the River Nile for exactly this reason.

Key word

Estuary — the wide, tidal mouth of a river where fresh river water meets salty sea water.

Our local rivers

The United Kingdom has hundreds of rivers. Together they form the veins of the country. Rivers gave us our oldest towns and cities — people always settled where they could drink, wash, fish, and travel.

Rivers · Key Vocabulary

SourceThe place where a river begins.
MouthWhere a river ends, often the sea.
TributaryA smaller river that joins a bigger one.
MeanderA big bend or curve in a river.
ErosionWearing the land away.
DepositionDropping sand and mud.
SedimentTiny bits of rock carried by water.
FloodplainFlat land near a river that sometimes floods.
RiverbedThe ground at the bottom of a river.
River bankThe ground on either side of a river.
LandformA natural shape on the Earth’s surface.
AgricultureFarming — growing food and keeping animals.
