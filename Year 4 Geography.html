Rivers and Streams

The journey of water from source to sea

What is a river?

A river is a large, flowing body of freshwater that moves across the land and usually empties into a sea, lake, or another river. A stream is a smaller flow of water — the kind you might be able to step across. Small streams join together to become bigger streams, and bigger streams join together to make rivers.

Rivers shape the world around us. They carve valleys, carry soil, grow farmland, and give homes to fish, birds, and insects. Every river tells a story of its journey from high ground to the open sea.

The water cycle

All the water in every river has been on a long trip — round and round, forever. This is called the water cycle.

  1. 1

    Evaporation

    The sun heats water in oceans, lakes, and rivers. The water turns into an invisible gas called water vapour and rises into the sky.

  2. 2

    Condensation

    High up, the vapour cools and turns back into tiny droplets. Billions of droplets together make a cloud.

  3. 3

    Precipitation

    When the droplets get too heavy, they fall as rain, snow, sleet, or hail.

  4. 4

    Collection

    The water trickles into streams and rivers, or soaks into the ground. Eventually it reaches the sea — and the cycle begins again.

The journey of a river

A river changes along its length. Geographers split its journey into three parts.

Upper course

Source

The source is where a river begins. Often it is high up in the hills or mountains, fed by rain, melting snow, or a spring. The water is cold, clear, and fast-flowing. It cuts a narrow V-shaped valley as it rushes downhill.

  • Fast water
  • V-shaped valleys
  • Waterfalls
Middle course

Tributaries and meanders

Other streams called tributaries join the river. Where two rivers meet is called a confluence. The river slows down and begins to swing from side to side, making wide loops called meanders.

  • Tributaries
  • Meanders
  • Oxbow lakes
Lower course

Mouth

The mouth is where the river meets the sea. The water is slow and wide, full of mud and sand it has carried from upstream. It may drop this load to build a fan-shaped delta, or open into a wide estuary.

  • Flood plains
  • Estuaries
  • Deltas

Key words

Source
The place where a river starts.
Tributary
A smaller river or stream that flows into a larger one.
Confluence
The meeting point of two rivers.
Meander
A curve or bend in a river.
Oxbow lake
A curved lake left behind when a meander is cut off from a river.
Waterfall
Water falling over an edge of hard rock.
Flood plain
The flat land next to a river that floods when the river is high.
Estuary
The wide part of a river where it meets the sea and saltwater mixes with fresh.
Delta
A flat, fan-shaped area of land at the mouth of a river, made of mud and sand.
Mouth
The place where a river ends.

Rivers of the world

The Nile

Africa · 6,650 km

One of the longest rivers in the world. It flows north through eleven countries and made farming possible in ancient Egypt.

The Amazon

South America · 6,400 km

Carries more water than any other river. Its rainforest is home to millions of plants and animals.

The Yangtze

Asia · 6,300 km

The longest river in Asia. It flows through China and is an important route for ships and trade.

The Thames

England · 346 km

Flows through London and past Windsor. It has been important for trade and travel for nearly two thousand years.

The Mississippi

United States · 3,770 km

The great river of central North America. It carries water from more than thirty states all the way to the Gulf of Mexico.

The Ganges

India · 2,525 km

Begins high in the Himalayan mountains and flows across northern India to a huge delta by the Bay of Bengal.

Why rivers matter

People have lived by rivers for as long as there have been people. Rivers give us drinking water, help us grow food, carry boats and cargo, turn the wheels of mills and power stations, and are homes for fish, otters, kingfishers, and dragonflies. Looking after our rivers — keeping them clean and healthy — helps everything that lives along them.
