Topic 1

Creation Stories

How the world began.

Every religion has a story about how the world began. The stories differ but each is a powerful answer to “where do we come from?”

Christian / Jewish (Genesis): God makes the world in six days — light, sky, sea and land, sun and moon, animals, then humans. On day seven God rests.

Hindu: the universe goes through cycles. Brahma creates it, Vishnu preserves it, Shiva eventually destroys it. Then it all begins again — over and over across billions of years.

Muslim (Qur’an): Allah creates the heavens and the earth in six days. Humans come from clay, shaped by God.

Science tells a different story — the Big Bang 13.8 billion years ago, then stars, then planets, then life. Many religious people today say the Big Bang IS how God did it — the two stories fit together.

Topic 2

Why is there Suffering?

One of the hardest questions.

Why do bad things happen to good people? Why are there wars, famines, illnesses? Every religion has wrestled with this.

Christian answer: humans were made free to choose, and sometimes we choose wrongly. Also, suffering can teach us patience and love. Jesus himself suffered on the cross.

Buddhist answer: suffering comes from wanting — always wanting more, always wanting things to be different. When you let go of wanting, you find peace. This is the First Noble Truth.

Hindu answer: karma. The way you act affects what happens to you next, in this life or a future life. Be kind and good things will come back to you.

None of these answers is easy. But thinking hard about suffering can make you more compassionate towards other people who are struggling.

Topic 3

Religious Leaders

The founders and prophets.

Religions usually start with one inspiring person.

  • Abraham — ancestor of Judaism, Christianity and Islam. Around 4,000 years ago.
  • Moses — Jewish prophet who led the people out of Egypt. About 3,300 years ago.
  • The Buddha (Siddhartha) — around 2,500 years ago, India.
  • Jesus — around 2,000 years ago, in the land now called Israel/Palestine.
  • Muhammad — around 1,400 years ago, in Arabia.
  • Guru Nanak — around 500 years ago, Punjab.

Each taught a new way of understanding the world. Each faced huge difficulty. Each is still studied by millions today.

Topic 4

Christmas in Depth

The story behind the cards.

You know Christmas already. Let’s look at the story carefully.

About 2,000 years ago, a young Jewish woman called Mary was engaged to a carpenter called Joseph. An angel told Mary she would have a special baby.

The Roman emperor ordered everyone to return to their home town for a count. Mary and Joseph travelled to Bethlehem. There was no room at any inn. They had to sleep in a stable. That’s where Jesus was born, laid in a manger (a feeding trough).

Shepherds came to see the baby because an angel told them. Three wise men (sometimes called kings or Magi) came from the East following a star. They brought gifts of gold, frankincense and myrrh.

Today Christians remember this story with nativity plays, carols, cards, church services — and giving each other presents.

Topic 5

Respecting Other Beliefs

Living together.

In Year 5 we think about what it means to respect people who believe differently. Britain is one of the most religiously mixed countries in the world. In a single classroom you might have Christians, Muslims, Hindus, Sikhs, Jews, Buddhists, and children whose families don’t practise any religion.

Respect doesn’t mean you have to AGREE with everything. It means:

  • Listening without making fun.
  • Asking questions to understand, not to tease.
  • Not assuming all Muslims/Hindus/Christians think the same — they don’t.
  • Noticing what we share as much as what’s different.

The UK law of 2010 (the Equality Act) makes it illegal to treat someone unfairly because of their religion. That’s a big idea, and a relatively new one.
