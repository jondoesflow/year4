Topic 1

Things That Are Special

Objects, people, places.

In Year 1 RE we start by thinking about what makes something special. You might have a special teddy, a special blanket, or a special place you like.

Lots of religions have special things too. Christians might treasure a Bible. Muslims treasure the Qur’an. Hindus often have a small shrine at home. These objects and places are special because they help people feel close to God or to something bigger than themselves.

Special doesn’t mean expensive. Special means it matters.

Topic 2

Christianity

A religion about Jesus.

Christianity is the biggest religion in the world. Christians believe in ONE God. They also believe that Jesus was God’s son, born as a baby about 2,000 years ago.

Christians go to a church to worship, sing, and pray. They celebrate:

  • Christmas — the birth of Jesus.
  • Easter — when Jesus came back to life after dying.

The Christian holy book is the Bible.

Topic 3

Judaism

The Jewish religion.

Jewish people believe in ONE God. Their religion is called Judaism. Jews go to a synagogue to pray. Their holy book is the Torah.

Jewish celebrations include:

  • Shabbat — a rest day every week, from Friday evening to Saturday evening.
  • Hanukkah — a winter festival of lights. A special candlestick called a menorah is lit over eight nights.
  • Passover (Pesach) — remembers when Jewish people escaped from slavery in Egypt long ago.
Topic 4

Religious Stories

Stories with a meaning.

Every religion has stories. The stories aren’t just fun — they teach people how to live.

Noah’s Ark (in the Bible and the Qur’an) — God tells Noah to build a giant boat and save two of every animal from a big flood. The rainbow at the end is a promise.

The Good Samaritan (a story Jesus told) — a man is hurt by robbers. Three people walk past. Only a stranger stops to help. The lesson: be kind to everyone, even people you don’t know.

Topic 5

Being Thankful

A big idea in every religion.

Most religions include saying thank you — thank you for food, thank you for family, thank you for being alive. Christians say grace before meals. Muslims say Bismillah (“In the name of God”) before eating.

You don’t need to be religious to feel grateful. Even noticing one thing you’re thankful for at bedtime helps you feel happier.
