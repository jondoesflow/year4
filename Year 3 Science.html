Topic 1

Plants

How a plant actually works.

A plant has these parts with specific jobs:

  • Roots — anchor the plant and suck up water and nutrients from the soil.
  • Stem — holds the plant up and is a tube carrying water up to the leaves.
  • Leaves — use sunlight to make food (photosynthesis).
  • Flower — makes seeds after being pollinated (usually by bees).

Life cycle: seed → seedling → grown plant → flowers → pollinated → seeds form → new plants grow.

Topic 2

Animals and Humans

Skeletons, muscles and nutrition.

Animals, including humans, need the right kinds and amounts of food. They cannot make their own food the way plants do.

Humans have a skeleton made of bones that:

  • Give us our shape.
  • Protect soft bits (ribs protect the heart and lungs; skull protects the brain).
  • Let us move, working with muscles.

Muscles are stretchy bits that pull on bones to make us move. They work in pairs.

Topic 3

Rocks & Fossils

Three kinds of rock, and how fossils form.

Rocks are sorted into three groups:

  • Sedimentary — made from layers of sand and mud pressed together (sandstone, limestone).
  • Igneous — made from cooled lava from volcanoes (granite, basalt).
  • Metamorphic — rocks changed by heat and pressure (marble, slate).

A fossil is the stony remains of a plant or animal that died millions of years ago. We learn about dinosaurs from fossils.

Topic 4

Light & Shadows

We see things because light bounces off them.

We need light to see. Light comes from the Sun, lamps, candles, torches, fire.

Shadows form when something blocks light. The closer you are to the light, the bigger the shadow.

Some materials are reflective (bounce light): mirrors, shiny metal. Others are opaque (block light): wood, you, a book.

Topic 5

Forces & Magnets

Push, pull, and the strange power of magnets.

A force is a push or a pull. Forces make things move, stop, or change direction.

Magnets attract (pull towards them) certain metals: iron, steel, nickel. They do NOT attract: plastic, wood, glass, aluminium, gold, copper.

Magnets have two ends called poles: North and South. Opposite poles attract. Same poles repel.
