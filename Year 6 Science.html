Topic 1

Classification

Sorting living things into groups.

Scientists sort living things into five kingdoms:

  1. Animals — all move and eat other living things.
  2. Plants — make their own food using sunlight.
  3. Fungi — mushrooms, mould, yeast.
  4. Protists — single-celled creatures.
  5. Bacteria — tiny living things.

Animals are further split: vertebrates (with backbones: fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds, mammals) and invertebrates (no backbone: insects, spiders, worms, molluscs).

The Linnaean system: scientists give every species a two-part Latin name. Humans = Homo sapiens.

Topic 2

The Circulatory System

How blood travels round your body.

Your circulatory system is your heart plus all your blood vessels. It delivers oxygen and food to every cell in your body.

Parts:

  • Heart — a muscular pump about the size of your fist.
  • Arteries — carry oxygen-rich blood AWAY from the heart.
  • Veins — carry blood BACK to the heart.
  • Capillaries — tiny vessels where oxygen and nutrients pass to cells.
  • Blood — the delivery fluid.

Exercise makes your heart beat faster because your muscles need more oxygen.

Things that harm your circulation: smoking, too much fat, no exercise. Things that help: exercise, healthy food, enough sleep.

Topic 3

Evolution & Inheritance

How living things change over millions of years.

Parents pass on characteristics to their children through genes. You might have your mum’s eyes and your grandad’s nose.

Over many, many generations, useful characteristics help living things survive. The ones that don’t survive don’t pass on their genes.

This slow change is called evolution. It was explained by Charles Darwin in his book On the Origin of Species (1859). He noticed on the Galapagos Islands that finches on different islands had different beak shapes, matching their food.

Fossils are evidence of evolution: we can see how creatures have changed over millions of years.

Topic 4

Light (in more detail)

How we see, and why light behaves oddly.

Light travels in straight lines. We see things because light bounces off them into our eyes.

Light can be:

  • Reflected — bounced off (mirrors).
  • Refracted — bent when it passes through water or glass. That’s why a straw in a glass of water looks broken.

Shadows form when an opaque object blocks light. The shape of a shadow matches the object.

To see a rainbow, you need sunlight and raindrops. Raindrops split white light into the seven colours of the rainbow: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet (remember: “Richard Of York Gave Battle In Vain”).

Topic 5

Electricity (in more detail)

Circuit symbols and what changes the brightness.

Electricity flows around a complete circuit. We draw circuits using standard symbols:

  • Cell (battery): a long line and a short line.
  • Bulb: a circle with a cross.
  • Switch: a gap that can open or close.
  • Wires: straight lines.
  • Motor: a circle with an M.
  • Buzzer: a D-shape.

Things that change how BRIGHT a bulb shines:

  • More cells (batteries) in series = brighter bulb.
  • More bulbs in series = dimmer each.
  • Longer wires = a tiny bit dimmer (more resistance).
