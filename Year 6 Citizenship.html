Topic 1

How a UK Election Works

Voting in detail.

The UK has a General Election at least every five years. It works like this:

  1. The PM (or Parliament) calls the election.
  2. Each political party picks ONE candidate per constituency. (A constituency is an area of about 70,000 voters.)
  3. Parties write a manifesto — a list of promises if they get elected.
  4. On election day, every adult goes to a polling station and votes for ONE candidate.
  5. Votes are counted overnight.
  6. The candidate with the most votes wins THEIR constituency — they become an MP.
  7. The party with the most MPs forms the government. Their leader becomes Prime Minister.

The UK’s main parties: Conservatives, Labour, Liberal Democrats, Greens, SNP (in Scotland), Plaid Cymru (in Wales), Reform UK.

Topic 2

How a Law Gets Made

From idea to rule.

Laws don’t just appear. They go through a long process:

  1. An MP (usually from the government) suggests a new law (called a Bill).
  2. The Bill gets debated and voted on in the Commons. MPs can change bits.
  3. If approved, it goes to the House of Lords. They debate and vote too.
  4. If both Houses agree, the Bill is sent to the King.
  5. The King signs it (Royal Assent).
  6. It becomes the law of the land.

The whole process can take months — sometimes years. That’s on purpose. Laws affect everyone, so we make sure they’re good before passing them.

Topic 3

Crime & Justice

When laws are broken.

If someone breaks the law, the police investigate. If they have enough evidence, the person goes to court.

In court:

  • The prosecution tries to prove the person is guilty.
  • The defence tries to show they’re innocent.
  • A judge runs the court.
  • For serious crimes, a jury of 12 ordinary people decides if the person is guilty or not.

If guilty, the judge decides the punishment: a fine, community service, or prison. UK law says people are innocent until proven guilty — a hugely important idea.

Topic 4

Climate Action

The biggest challenge.

Climate change is probably the biggest challenge your generation will face. The Earth is getting warmer because we’re burning fossil fuels (coal, oil, gas) that release carbon dioxide.

Effects already happening:

  • Hotter summers, milder winters.
  • More extreme weather (storms, floods, droughts).
  • Polar ice melting.
  • Sea levels rising.
  • Animals losing their habitats.

What people are doing about it:

  • Switching to renewable energy (wind, solar).
  • Electric cars.
  • Eating less meat.
  • Flying less.
  • Planting trees.

Young people like Greta Thunberg have led huge worldwide protests demanding faster action.

Topic 5

Active Citizenship

You can change things.

You don’t have to wait until you’re 18 to make a difference. Children and young people have changed the world.

  • Malala Yousafzai — from age 11 fought for girls’ education in Pakistan. Survived being shot. Won the Nobel Peace Prize at 17.
  • Greta Thunberg — from age 15 led the worldwide school climate strike movement.
  • Marcus Rashford — footballer, but used his voice to make sure poor children got school meals during the school holidays.

Things you can do now:

  • Stand for school council.
  • Write to your local MP about something you care about.
  • Start a campaign at school (less plastic, kinder lunchtimes).
  • Volunteer at a charity event.
  • Just be kind, every day, to everyone. That changes things too.
