Topic 1

Counting to 100

Numbers are like friends — they love standing in order.

In Year 2 we count all the way to 100 and back again.

We can count in different ways:

  • In ones: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5…
  • In twos: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12…
  • In fives: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25…
  • In tens: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50…

Every two-digit number has a tens place and a ones place. In 47, the 4 means 4 tens (40) and the 7 means 7 ones. 40 + 7 = 47.

Tens and ones

The number 23 is 2 tens and 3 ones.

The number 68 is 6 tens and 8 ones.

Tip: the left digit is ALWAYS the tens.

Topic 2

Adding & Subtracting

Add means put together. Subtract means take away.

To add two numbers, count on: 7 + 5. Start at 7, then count 5 more: 8, 9, 10, 11, 12. The answer is 12.

To subtract, count back: 15 − 4. Start at 15, go back 4: 14, 13, 12, 11. The answer is 11.

A good trick is to partition bigger numbers into tens and ones:

  • 23 + 14. Tens: 20 + 10 = 30. Ones: 3 + 4 = 7. Put it back: 30 + 7 = 37.
  • Doubles are quick: 6 + 6 = 12. 8 + 8 = 16. Learn them off by heart.
Number bonds to 10

These pairs always add to 10. Learn them by heart:

  • 0 + 10
  • 1 + 9
  • 2 + 8
  • 3 + 7
  • 4 + 6
  • 5 + 5
Topic 3

2, 5 and 10 Times Tables

Three tables to learn off by heart in Year 2.

Times tables are just clever counting. The 2 times table is counting in twos. The 5 times table is counting in fives. The 10 times table is counting in tens.

Tips:

  • Anything times 1 is itself. 7 × 1 = 7.
  • Anything times 0 is 0. 7 × 0 = 0.
  • 3 × 5 is the same as 5 × 3. The order doesn’t matter.
  • Every number in the 10 times table ends in a 0.
  • Every number in the 5 times table ends in a 5 or a 0.
The 5 times table
  • 1 × 5 = 5
  • 2 × 5 = 10
  • 3 × 5 = 15
  • 4 × 5 = 20
  • 5 × 5 = 25
  • 6 × 5 = 30
  • 7 × 5 = 35
  • 8 × 5 = 40
  • 9 × 5 = 45
  • 10 × 5 = 50
Topic 4

Halves & Quarters

Sharing a whole into equal parts.

A fraction is a slice of a whole.

  • 1/2 means one half. Share something in 2 EQUAL parts and take one part.
  • 1/4 means one quarter. Share into 4 equal parts, take one.
  • 3/4 means three quarters. Share into 4 equal parts, take three.
  • 1/3 means one third. Share into 3 equal parts, take one.

To find half of a number, share it into two equal groups:

  • Half of 10 is 5 (5 + 5 = 10).
  • Half of 20 is 10.
  • Half of 8 is 4.
Topic 5

Money & Time

Coins, notes, and the hands of a clock.

Coins you’ll see: 1p, 2p, 5p, 10p, 20p, 50p, £1, £2. Notes: £5, £10, £20, £50.

100 pence = £1.

Practice:

  • Five 10p coins = 50p.
  • Two 20p coins and a 10p coin = 50p.
  • Four 5p coins = 20p.

Time. In Year 2 we read clocks to the quarter hour:

  • O’clock — the minute hand points straight up (12).
  • Quarter past — minute hand on the 3.
  • Half past — minute hand on the 6.
  • Quarter to — minute hand on the 9.
Days, weeks and months
  • 7 days in a week
  • 12 months in a year
  • 24 hours in a day
  • 60 minutes in an hour
  • 60 seconds in a minute
Topic 6

Shapes

Flat shapes and solid shapes — name them, spot them, count their sides.

2D shapes are FLAT. You can draw them on paper:

  • Triangle — 3 sides, 3 corners.
  • Square — 4 equal sides, 4 corners.
  • Rectangle — 4 sides (two long, two short), 4 corners.
  • Pentagon — 5 sides.
  • Hexagon — 6 sides.
  • Circle — no straight sides, no corners.

3D shapes are SOLID. You can hold them:

  • Cube — like a dice. 6 square faces.
  • Cuboid — like a cereal box.
  • Sphere — a ball.
  • Cylinder — a tin of beans.
  • Pyramid — pointy top, square or triangular bottom.
  • Cone — like an ice-cream cone.
