Topic 1

Life Cycles

How living things grow and reproduce.

Every living thing has a life cycle:

  • Mammals: baby → child → adult → old age. Mammals give birth to live babies and feed them milk.
  • Birds: egg → chick → adult bird.
  • Amphibians (e.g. frogs): egg → tadpole → froglet → frog.
  • Insects: egg → larva (caterpillar) → pupa (chrysalis) → adult (butterfly). This big change is called metamorphosis.
  • Plants: seed → seedling → plant → flower → pollinated → seeds → new plants.
Topic 2

Humans: Growing Up

The stages of a human life.

Humans go through these stages:

  1. Baby (0 – 2 years)
  2. Child (2 – about 11)
  3. Adolescent (roughly 11 – 19)
  4. Adult
  5. Old age

In adolescence the body changes a lot — this is called puberty. It prepares the body to become an adult.

Topic 3

Properties of Materials

What makes materials useful, and how they change.

Materials have different properties: hardness, transparency, conductivity (do they carry electricity or heat?), magnetic or not, soluble or not.

Reversible changes: can be undone. Freezing water into ice; dissolving salt in water (evaporate the water, salt comes back).

Irreversible changes: cannot be undone. Burning paper; baking a cake; mixing vinegar and bicarb (they make a new substance).

Topic 4

Forces

Gravity, friction, air resistance, water resistance.

A force is a push or a pull. Year 5 forces include:

  • Gravity — pulls everything towards the centre of the Earth. Discovered by Sir Isaac Newton when he watched an apple fall.
  • Friction — slows things down when they rub against each other.
  • Air resistance — air pushes back against a moving thing. Parachutes use it.
  • Water resistance — same idea, but in water. Boats and fish are streamlined to reduce it.

Simple machines that use forces: levers, pulleys, gears.

Topic 5

Earth & Space

The Sun, the planets, day and night.

Our Solar System has one star (the Sun) and eight planets, in order from the Sun:

Mercury · Venus · Earth · Mars · Jupiter · Saturn · Uranus · Neptune

Memory trick: “My Very Easy Method Just Speeds Up Naming.”

The Earth spins once every 24 hours. That’s why we have day and night.

The Earth orbits (goes around) the Sun once every 365 and a bit days. That’s a year.

The Moon orbits the Earth once every 28 days.
