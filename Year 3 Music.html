Topic 1

Reading Music

Notes on a stave.

Music is written on a stave — five horizontal lines. Notes sit ON the lines or IN the spaces between them. Where a note sits on the stave tells you its pitch.

At the start of a stave is a clef — a fancy symbol. The treble clef (it looks like a fancy G) is used for higher-pitched instruments.

Notes have names: A, B, C, D, E, F, G. After G, we go back to A again. It’s like a loop.

Topic 2

How Long a Note Lasts

Short and long notes.

Some notes are long, some are short. The shape of the note tells you how long to play it:

  • Semibreve (whole note) — an open oval. 4 beats.
  • Minim (half note) — open oval with a stem. 2 beats.
  • Crotchet (quarter note) — filled oval with a stem. 1 beat.
  • Quaver (eighth note) — filled oval with a flag. Half a beat.
Topic 3

Playing the Recorder

A classic first instrument.

Many Year 3 classes learn the recorder. It’s small, cheap, and surprisingly versatile.

Basics:

  • Hold it in both hands — left at the top, right at the bottom (in most English schools).
  • Cover all the holes you need with pads of your fingers.
  • Blow GENTLY. Screechy squawks mean too much breath.
  • Use your tongue to separate each note — “too, too, too”.
Topic 4

Singing in Rounds

Starting at different times.

A round is a song where one group starts singing, and then another group starts the same song a few beats LATER. They overlap, creating layered harmonies.

Row, Row, Row Your Boat is a famous round. London’s Burning and Frere Jacques are others. Year 3 tries singing in rounds with two or three groups.

Topic 5

Famous Composers

People who wrote classics.

Year 3 composers to know:

  • Mozart (1756–1791) — Austrian child prodigy. Wrote symphonies at age 8.
  • Beethoven (1770–1827) — German. Wrote his best music after he went DEAF — by imagining it.
  • John Williams (born 1932) — American. Composer of the music for Star Wars, Harry Potter, Jurassic Park, and many more films.
