Topic 1

Relative Clauses

Adding extra information to a noun.

A relative clause is a group of words starting with who, which, that, whose, whom, where, when. It adds information about a noun.

Examples:

  • The girl who lives next door is my cousin.
  • The book that Sam gave me was brilliant.
  • The shop, which opened last week, sells cakes.

If the relative clause is extra (not essential), put it in commas.

Topic 2

Modal Verbs

Words that show how likely or possible something is.

Modal verbs change what a verb means. The main ones are:

can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, would

Examples:

  • I can swim. (I’m able to.)
  • You might see a fox. (It’s possible.)
  • You must wear a coat. (It’s necessary.)
  • We should leave soon. (It’s a good idea.)
Topic 3

Parenthesis & Brackets

Adding extra information, the neat way.

Parenthesis is extra information you add to a sentence. You can mark it with:

  • Brackets: My cat (who is called Patch) is fluffy.
  • Dashes: My cat — who is called Patch — is fluffy.
  • Commas: My cat, who is called Patch, is fluffy.

The sentence must still make sense if you remove the parenthesis.

Topic 4

Cohesion

Joining ideas smoothly.

Cohesion is about making writing flow. We use:

  • Adverbials: First, Next, After that, Meanwhile, Finally.
  • Pronouns: He, she, it, they — to avoid repeating names.
  • Synonyms: using different words for the same idea.

Example: Lily took the lead. She ran so fast that soon after, Ravi was far behind.

Topic 5

Formal & Informal

Different styles for different readers.

Informal writing is chatty and relaxed. It’s for texts to friends, diary entries, notes to family.

Formal writing is tidy and polite. It’s for letters to strangers, reports, emails to a teacher or a shop.

Formal tips: full words (don’t use contractions like “don’t” — use “do not”), no slang, use polite language (“please”, “thank you”, “I would like”).
