Topic 1

Research

Looking at the world.

Year 5 research is serious. Before designing, you:

  • Investigate existing products in depth.
  • Interview your users. What do they actually need?
  • Look at materials, costs, sustainability.
  • Write a clear design brief — what the product must do.
  • Draft a specification — a list of requirements (size, weight, strength, colour, cost).
Topic 2

Programming Products

Code meets DT.

Modern products have computers inside them. Year 5 can bring programming into DT projects using devices like:

  • Micro:bit — a tiny computer you can program to flash lights, play sounds, and react to input.
  • Crumble controller — simple and robust, good for schools.
  • Makey Makey — lets any conductive object become a button.

Combined with a handmade shell, you can build interactive products: a reaction-timer game, a night light, a distance sensor.

Topic 3

Gears & Pulleys

Transferring motion.

Gears are toothed wheels that mesh together. When one turns, the other turns too — often at a different speed. A big gear turning a small gear = the small gear goes fast. A small gear turning a big gear = the big gear goes slowly but with more force.

That’s why bikes have gears. Low gear (small front, big back) = easy to pedal uphill but slow. High gear (big front, small back) = hard to pedal but fast on flat.

Pulley systems work similarly but with belts or ropes over wheels.

Topic 4

Cooking & Nutrition

Understanding what we eat.

Year 5 connects cooking to nutrition. Every food has a mix of:

  • Carbohydrates — energy. Bread, pasta, potatoes.
  • Protein — growth and repair. Meat, fish, beans, eggs.
  • Fats — energy, warmth, protecting organs.
  • Vitamins & minerals — tiny amounts of many things that help our bodies work.
  • Fibre — helps digestion.
  • Water.

Year 5 cooking: multi-step recipes, more independent kitchen skills, exploring dishes from other cultures.

Topic 5

Evaluating

Peer review and self-critique.

Year 5 evaluation gets rigorous:

  • Test your product with real users. Ask them honest questions.
  • Measure how well it met the original specification — point by point.
  • Think about sustainability — could it be recycled? Made of greener materials?
  • Consider cost — could it be made more cheaply?
