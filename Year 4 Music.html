Topic 1

Reading Music

The stave in more detail.

In Year 4 we read music more confidently. The lines of a treble clef stave, from bottom to top, are:

E – G – B – D – F. Remember: Every Good Boy Deserves Fudge.

The spaces are: F – A – C – E — which conveniently spells FACE.

A bar is a group of beats, separated by vertical bar lines. Most songs have 4 beats per bar.

A time signature at the start tells you. 4/4 means 4 beats per bar, each a crotchet.

Topic 2

Orchestral Instruments

Getting to know the orchestra.

A full orchestra has about 80 players. Instruments are grouped by family and seated in a pattern, with strings at the front (closest to the conductor) and percussion at the back (loudest).

Each family has a range from low to high. In the strings: double bass (lowest), cello, viola, violin (highest). In brass: tuba (lowest), trombone, French horn, trumpet (highest).

The conductor stands in front, facing the orchestra, keeping everyone in time with a baton.

Topic 3

Composing

Writing your own piece.

Year 4 composing means writing longer, more structured pieces. Tips:

  • Start and end on the same note (the key note) — it feels finished.
  • Try a simple repeating pattern (an ostinato) as backing.
  • Use mostly stepwise movement — next-door notes sound better than big leaps.
  • Include some repetition — repeat a short phrase before going somewhere new.
Topic 4

Music Through Time

Eras of Western music.

Western music history has big eras:

  • Baroque (1600-1750) — ornate, fancy. Bach, Vivaldi, Handel.
  • Classical (1750-1820) — balanced, structured. Mozart, Haydn, early Beethoven.
  • Romantic (1820-1900) — emotional, big orchestras. Tchaikovsky, Wagner.
  • 20th Century (1900-2000) — anything goes. Jazz, blues, rock, pop, electronic music.
  • 21st Century (now) — the same but more.
Topic 5

Composers

Three more.

Year 4 composers:

  • Johann Sebastian Bach (1685-1750) — German Baroque master. Wrote hundreds of pieces.
  • Antonio Vivaldi (1678-1741) — Italian. Wrote the Four Seasons — four violin concertos representing the seasons.
  • Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky (1840-1893) — Russian. Composed the Nutcracker, Swan Lake, and 1812 Overture.
