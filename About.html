About the site

Made for Tilly & Esme

A small, free, private learning website built by a dad for his two daughters — one in Key Stage 1, one in Key Stage 2 — with every page designed to be easy on dyslexic eyes.

Chapter 1

Two curious sisters

This whole site exists because of two little people.

I’m Jon. I’m a proud dad of two very curious daughters, and this little website is for them.

Tilly is in Year 2. She’s in Key Stage 1, so she’s learning to count to a hundred, spot every letter sound, tell the time to the quarter hour, and name the four countries of the UK. She loves plants, drawing, and asking “why?” more than any other word.

Esme is in Year 4. She’s in Key Stage 2, working on her times tables, fronted adverbials, rivers of the world, and the Ancient Maya. Esme is dyslexic, which means reading takes more effort for her than it does for most people — but once she’s into a topic, she remembers details that amaze me.

Every page on this site is written with both of them in mind. Tilly gets the Year 2 side. Esme gets the Year 4 side. And everything — the fonts, the colours, the line spacing, the read-aloud buttons — is chosen to make reading kinder on dyslexic eyes.

Quick map

  • Esme (Year 4) → the Year 4 section, Key Stage 2.
  • Both get read-aloud, dyslexia-friendly fonts, friendly chapters, and fresh practice questions on every visit.
Chapter 2

Why I built it

Esme came home one evening last term, put her book bag down, and said something that stayed with me: “The words are too squashed up, Daddy, and they swim about.”

There are brilliant dyslexia-friendly books and apps out there. There’s also a LOT of homework, revision, and holiday recap that arrives as dense photocopies or tiny-text websites that make dyslexic kids feel stupid when they’re anything but.

So I started building this site. The goal was small and specific: take the UK Key Stage 1 and Key Stage 2 National Curriculum, write it up as short friendly chapters, and make every page easy to read, easy to listen to, and easy to come back to. No logins. No ads. No pop-ups. No paywall. Nothing selling to my kids.

Tilly saw the first page over my shoulder and immediately demanded her own section. That’s how Year 2 happened.

Chapter 3

What you’ll find on the site

Both year groups are built the same way. Pick a year, pick a subject, and you’ll find friendly chapters covering the UK curriculum for that age.

  • Maths — counting, times tables, shapes, money, and loads of practice.
  • English — phonics, grammar, spelling, reading, and writing.
  • Science — plants, animals, the human body, materials, states of matter, sound, electricity.
  • History — the Great Fire of London, Florence Nightingale, the Romans, the Vikings, the Ancient Maya, and more.
  • Geography — the UK, the continents, rivers, climate, and how to read a map.

Every page has:

  • A Read-to-me button that speaks the chapter out loud in a calm British voice.
  • Little word helpers — tap or hover on an underlined word and a plain-English meaning pops up.
  • A Test Yourself quiz at the end. Maths and English quizzes give you brand new questions every visit, so the practice never goes stale.
Deep dives

Two longer guidebooks live inside the Year 4 History and Geography sections:

  • The Ancient Maya — eleven chapters, a timeline, and a proper illustrated quiz.
  • Rivers — six lessons from a mountain spring to the open sea.
Chapter 4

Designed for dyslexic readers

Building this alongside Esme, I made a short list of rules I wouldn’t break:

  • Atkinson Hyperlegible for the body text — a font from the Braille Institute, designed specifically to make letters harder to confuse.
  • Lexend for headings — designed to improve reading fluency for people with dyslexia.
  • Cream background, not white. White paper glares; cream calms the eye.
  • Generous line height and extra letter-spacing — so words stop “squashing up”.
  • Left-aligned text only. No justified blocks that leave rivers of white space running down the page.
  • Short paragraphs. Lots of bullet lists. Clear single-idea sentences.
  • Read-to-me on every chapter, every time — so reading can be listened to when the eyes are tired.
  • No moving text, no flashing buttons, no auto-playing anything.

It’s not perfect. I’ll keep tweaking as Esme tells me what helps and what doesn’t.

Chapter 5

Free, private, and independent

This site is totally free for Tilly, Esme, and anyone else who stumbles across it.

  • No accounts. Nobody has to sign up for anything.
  • No adverts. Nobody is selling anything to your kid.
  • No tracking cookies. No third-party pixels. No Google Analytics.
  • A tiny privacy-friendly visitor counter (Umami) tells me how many people visit and which pages they read. It doesn’t track individuals.

I built it. I pay for it. I decide what goes on it. That’s the deal.

What we don’t do

No ads. No pop-ups. No accounts. No newsletters. No “sign up for our premium tier”. No dark patterns.

Chapter 6

Where the content comes from

Everything on this site is aligned to the UK National Curriculum — specifically the Key Stage 1 and Key Stage 2 programmes of study published by the Department for Education. You can read the original at gov.uk.

I’ve tried to cover the topics that actually appear in Year 2 and Year 4 classrooms — not every tiny strand, but the ones Tilly and Esme are likely to meet this year.

The wording is my own, rewritten to be simple enough for a six-year-old or a nine-year-old to read (or listen to) without help. If you spot a mistake, please let me know — there will be some.

Chapter 7

Who this is for

First and foremost, it’s for Tilly and Esme.

But if you’ve found it because your own child is in Year 2 or Year 4 at a UK primary, or because they find reading hard, or because you’d like a gentle homework top-up that doesn’t feel like a test — please use it. Share it. Bookmark it. It’s yours too.

It works just as well on a tablet as on a laptop. Esme mostly uses it on the sofa on an iPad. Tilly likes tapping the Read-to-me button and then pretending she’s reading along.
