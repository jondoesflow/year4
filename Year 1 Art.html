Topic 1

Drawing & Mark Making

Pencils, crayons, and the shapes they leave.

Drawing is one of the oldest things humans do. The cave paintings in France are 40,000 years old. People have always wanted to put marks down and show what they see.

In Year 1 we try lots of different mark-making tools: pencils (hard lines, soft lines), crayons (wax, bright colours), chalk (smudgy, easy to blend), felt tips (bold and bright). Each tool makes a different kind of mark.

Try making lots of different kinds of lines: thick, thin, wavy, straight, zig-zag, spiral, dotted. Lines are the building blocks of drawing.

Topic 2

Colour

The rainbow, and how to mix it.

There are three primary colours — red, yellow, blue. They’re called primary because you can’t mix any other colours to make them.

If you mix two primaries, you get a secondary colour:

  • Red + yellow = orange
  • Yellow + blue = green
  • Red + blue = purple

And if you add white, colours get lighter. Add black, colours get darker. Painters are always mixing colours to find just the right shade.

Topic 3

Painting

Brushes, paper, and patience.

In Year 1 we paint with water-based paints. They’re forgiving — if you spill, it wipes up easily.

Tips:

  • Put newspaper or a mat down first.
  • Dip the brush lightly — too much paint drips.
  • Wash the brush between colours.
  • Let one layer dry before painting over it.

Painters use different sizes of brush: a big brush for large areas, a thin one for details. Your kitchen sponge is also a painting tool — press it in paint and dab it on paper for a brilliant textured effect.

Topic 4

Sculpture

Making art you can hold.

Sculpture is 3D art. Instead of flat on paper, you can walk around it.

In Year 1 we might make sculptures from:

  • Clay or plasticine — squish, pinch, roll.
  • Junk modelling — bits of cardboard, yogurt pots, cereal boxes, held together with tape.
  • Salt dough — flour, salt and water baked hard.
Topic 5

Famous Artists

People whose pictures filled the world.

Year 1 meets a few artists from history:

  • Vincent Van Gogh (1853 – 1890) — Dutch painter. Famous for sunflowers and starry skies. He painted with thick, swirling brush-strokes.
  • Henri Matisse (1869 – 1954) — French artist. When he got too old to paint, he made huge pictures by cutting out coloured paper.
  • Yayoi Kusama (born 1929) — Japanese artist famous for dots. Lots and lots of colourful dots everywhere.
