Topic 1

Pulse & Rhythm

Two ideas that are not quite the same.

Pulse and rhythm sound like the same thing but they’re different.

The pulse is the steady beat underneath. One, two, one, two. Even if no one’s singing, the pulse keeps going.

The rhythm is the actual pattern of long and short notes that the tune makes. In Happy Birthday, the rhythm goes: ta-ta-ta-aaa-ta-aaa… while the pulse is a steady tick-tick-tick underneath.

Topic 2

High & Low Notes

Pitch.

Notes can be high or low. A tiny mouse squeaks with HIGH notes. A big bear growls with LOW notes. This is pitch — how high or low a sound is.

On a xylophone, the longer bars play LOW and the shorter bars play HIGH. Instruments follow this rule: longer = lower. A big double bass plays low, a tiny violin plays high.

Topic 3

Instrument Families

The orchestra.

Instruments split into four families, based on how they make sound:

  • Strings — you pluck or bow a stretched string. Violin, cello, double bass, guitar, harp.
  • Woodwind — blow through a tube. Flute, recorder, clarinet, oboe.
  • Brass — buzz your lips into a mouthpiece. Trumpet, trombone, French horn.
  • Percussion — hit, shake or scrape. Drums, cymbals, xylophone, triangle.

An orchestra has all four families playing together.

Topic 4

Composing

Making up your own music.

A composer makes up new music. In Year 2 we try composing our own short pieces.

Start simple. Pick four notes on a xylophone. Make up a short pattern. Play it. Does it sound interesting? Try changing one note. Does it sound better?

You can also compose with words: make up a chant or a rap. Rhythm + words = music.

Topic 5

Music From Everywhere

Different places, different sounds.

Different parts of the world have their own kinds of music:

  • African drumming — complex rhythms, lots of drummers playing together.
  • Indian classical music — the sitar (a long stringed instrument) and tabla (hand drums).
  • Reggae from Jamaica — laid-back beats with a strong off-beat.
  • Scottish pipes — bagpipes, a proper traditional sound.
