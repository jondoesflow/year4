Topic 1

User-Centred Design

Who am I making this for?

By Year 6, design is all about the USER. Real designers don’t build what THEY want — they build what the user needs.

User-centred design steps:

  1. Understand the user. Who are they? What’s their daily life like?
  2. Interview or observe them.
  3. Define the problem you’re solving.
  4. Brainstorm lots of ideas.
  5. Prototype — a rough version to test.
  6. Test with real users. Learn what works.
  7. Iterate — try again, better.
Topic 2

Electronics & Programming

Smart products.

Year 6 combines electronics and programming. Your product can sense and respond:

  • Input sensors: button, light sensor, temperature sensor, microphone.
  • Microcontroller: a tiny computer like the micro:bit or Arduino.
  • Output devices: LED, buzzer, motor, servo.

Example: a plant-watering system. Sensor checks soil moisture. If soil is dry, the microcontroller turns on a pump. Programming decides what happens when.

Topic 3

Choosing Materials

The right stuff for the job.

Every material has properties that make it good or bad for a job. Wood is strong but heavy. Plastic is light and cheap but not great for the environment. Metal is very strong but hard to cut. Cardboard is cheap and light but weak.

Choosing is always a balance:

  • Strength — will it hold up?
  • Weight — does it need to be portable?
  • Cost — can the user afford it?
  • Environmental impact — is it renewable? Recyclable? Biodegradable?
  • Appearance — does it look right?
Topic 4

Cooking for Purpose

Full-course meals.

Year 6 cooks with genuine skill. You can plan and cook a whole meal: starter, main, dessert. You understand nutrition, seasonality, and dietary needs (vegetarian, vegan, allergies).

Advanced skills:

  • Cooking a balanced main course with protein, carbs and veg.
  • Using a hob and oven confidently.
  • Timing multiple things so they’re all ready together.
  • Presenting food attractively.
Topic 5

Evaluating

The full picture.

Final-year evaluation is comprehensive. You assess your product against:

  • The brief — did it achieve what was asked?
  • Users — did they like it? What did they struggle with?
  • Specification — did it meet the requirements?
  • Sustainability — how green is it? Could it be greener?
  • Cost — could it be produced cheaper? Sold for a fair price?
  • Personal growth — what did YOU learn? What skills are you still working on?
