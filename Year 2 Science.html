Topic 1

Plants

Tiny seeds grow into big trees. What do plants need to live?

Most plants grow from a seed or a bulb. A seed needs to be planted in soil and watered to wake up and grow.

All plants need four things to stay alive:

  • Water — through their roots.
  • Light — from the Sun.
  • Air — they breathe it in through their leaves.
  • Warmth — they don’t like being too cold.

A plant has these parts:

  • Roots — hold the plant in the ground and drink up water.
  • Stem — holds the plant up and carries water to the leaves.
  • Leaves — catch the sunlight to make food.
  • Flower — the pretty part. It turns into fruit and new seeds.
Try this at home

Put a bean on damp cotton wool in a jar. Leave it somewhere sunny and keep the cotton wool wet. In about a week you’ll see roots pushing down and a tiny shoot pushing up. That’s a brand new plant!

Topic 2

Animals, including Humans

All animals — including YOU — need the same few things.

Every animal, from an ant to an elephant, needs:

  • Water to drink.
  • Food to eat.
  • Air to breathe.
  • A safe home to live in.

Most baby animals look a bit like their parents. A puppy grows into a dog. A tadpole grows into a frog. A human baby grows into an adult over many years.

To stay healthy, humans need:

  • To eat the right amount of different food types.
  • To exercise — running, jumping, playing.
  • To get plenty of sleep.
  • Good hygiene — wash hands, brush teeth, have a bath.
Food groups
  • Fruit & veg — lots of this.
  • Starchy food — bread, pasta, potatoes.
  • Protein — fish, meat, beans, eggs.
  • Dairy — milk, cheese, yoghurt.
  • Sugary / fatty food — just a little bit!
Topic 3

Habitats

A habitat is the home where a plant or animal lives.

Different animals live in different places because they’re SUITED to them:

  • Woodland — squirrels, foxes, badgers, owls, oak trees.
  • Pond — frogs, fish, ducks, pondweed, lily pads.
  • Ocean — fish, crabs, whales, seaweed.
  • Desert — camels, snakes, cacti (plants that store water).
  • Polar — polar bears, penguins, seals, ice and snow.

A smaller home inside a habitat is called a micro-habitat. Lift up a stone or a log and you’ll find worms, woodlice and beetles — that’s their micro-habitat.

A tiny food chain

leaf → caterpillar → bird

The arrow means "is eaten by". The leaf is eaten by the caterpillar. The caterpillar is eaten by the bird.

Topic 4

Everyday Materials

Everything around you is made of something. What is it made of?

Here are the everyday materials you see all the time:

  • Wood — from trees. Tables, pencils, floors.
  • Plastic — toys, bottles, bags.
  • Metal — spoons, coins, bikes, keys.
  • Glass — windows, drinking glasses, jars.
  • Paper — books, drawings, boxes.
  • Fabric — clothes, curtains, blankets.
  • Rubber — tyres, balls, elastic bands.

Materials can be:

  • Squashed — like clay or a sponge.
  • Bent — like a pipe cleaner.
  • Twisted — like a tea towel being wrung out.
  • Stretched — like an elastic band.

Topic 5

The Seasons

The year rolls around in four seasons.

The Earth travels around the Sun. As it does, different parts of the world get more or less sunlight. That’s why we have four seasons:

  • Spring (March, April, May) — plants grow. Baby animals are born. The days get longer.
  • Summer (June, July, August) — hot and sunny. The longest days of the year.
  • Autumn (September, October, November) — leaves change colour and fall from the trees.
  • Winter (December, January, February) — cold. The shortest days. Sometimes snow!

The weather is what it’s like outside TODAY — sunny, rainy, windy, snowy, cloudy. The season is what TIME OF YEAR it is.
