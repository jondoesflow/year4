Topic 1

Healthy Friendships

What good ones look like.

Year 3 friendships get more complicated. Some friendships are healthy — they make both people feel good. Some are unhealthy.

Healthy:

  • You feel happy after seeing each other.
  • You both talk and both listen.
  • You can disagree without falling out for long.
  • You’re still friends with other people too.

Unhealthy:

  • One of you bosses the other.
  • You’re scared of upsetting them.
  • They get cross if you play with anyone else.
  • You feel worse after you’ve hung out.

If a friendship feels unhealthy, talk to a grown-up. You’re allowed to step back from a friendship.

Topic 2

Big Feelings

Calming down.

Sometimes feelings feel HUGE — too big for your body. There are tricks to calm them down:

  • Belly breathing — breathe in slowly through your nose for 4 seconds. Out for 6 seconds. Repeat 5 times.
  • 5-4-3-2-1 — name 5 things you can SEE, 4 you can HEAR, 3 you can TOUCH, 2 you can SMELL, 1 you can TASTE.
  • Cold water — splash cold water on your face. It resets your nervous system.
  • Move — run round the garden, do star jumps. Movement burns off stress.
  • Tell someone — saying it out loud often shrinks it.
Topic 3

Online Safety

Smart and safe.

The internet is brilliant for learning, watching, and talking with people. It’s also where some unsafe stuff happens. Year 3 rules:

  • Don’t share personal stuff online — your real name (full), your school, your address, your phone, photos.
  • Talk to grown-ups about who you message. If you don’t know someone in real life, they shouldn’t be a friend online.
  • Tell a grown-up if anything makes you uncomfortable. Even if you think you’ll be in trouble. You won’t.
  • If a website asks for personal info, leave.
  • Have screen-free time. Your brain needs breaks from screens.
Topic 4

Respecting Differences

We’re not all the same.

People are different in lots of ways:

  • Race and skin colour.
  • Religion.
  • Family setup (one parent, two parents, two mums, two dads).
  • Disability — some people use wheelchairs, hearing aids, glasses, sticks, or learn differently.
  • Language — some people speak two or three languages at home.

The UK Equality Act 2010 says it’s ILLEGAL to treat someone unfairly because of who they are. Treating people kindly, asking polite questions to learn, never name-calling — that’s how we live well together.

Topic 5

Sleep & Exercise

Why your body needs both.

Your body NEEDS sleep and exercise the way it needs food and water.

Sleep:

  • 7-year-olds need 9–11 hours a night.
  • Sleep heals your body and locks in what you learned that day.
  • Without sleep, you feel grumpy and find it hard to concentrate.
  • Screens before bed make sleep worse — stop screens an hour before bedtime.

Exercise:

  • Aim for at least 60 minutes a day.
  • Walking, cycling, running, climbing, swimming, dancing all count.
  • Exercise makes you happier, healthier, and helps you sleep better.
