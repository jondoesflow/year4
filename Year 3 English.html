Topic 1

Prefixes & Suffixes

Word-parts stuck on the start or end.

A prefix goes at the start and changes the meaning:

  • un- = not: unhappy, unkind, unsafe.
  • dis- = not / opposite: disagree, disappear.
  • re- = again: redo, rewrite.
  • pre- = before: preheat, preview.
  • sub- = under: submarine, subway.
  • super- = above, best: superstar, supermarket.

A suffix goes at the end: -ly (quickly), -ful (careful), -less (careless), -ness (kindness), -ment (movement).

Topic 2

Paragraphs

Grouping ideas together.

A paragraph is a group of sentences about ONE idea. When the idea changes, start a new paragraph.

Each new paragraph starts on a new line, often with a small indent.

Topic 3

Speech Marks

Showing what someone says.

When you write what someone says, put inverted commas (speech marks) around their words:

“I am hungry,” said Mia.

Rules: capital letter to start. Punctuation inside the closing mark. Comma before “said” if the speech comes first.

Topic 4

Expanded Noun Phrases

Adding juicy words to a noun.

A plain noun: the dog. An expanded noun phrase adds describing words to paint a picture:

  • the scruffy brown dog
  • the huge old stone castle
  • a bright red shiny apple
Topic 5

Poetry

Playing with the sound and shape of words.

A poem is a little bit of writing that plays with sound, rhythm, and pictures.

Rhyming words sound the same at the end: cat / hat / bat.

Alliteration: words that start with the same sound in a row: silly slippery snakes.
