Topic 1

Drawing from Observation

Looking carefully at the world.

In Year 4 we learn to draw from observation — looking at something in front of us and putting it on paper. Not making it up, not copying a picture — drawing what we actually see.

The trick is to LOOK MORE than you draw. Spend lots of time just noticing — the way the light falls, the small details, the shapes between things (called negative space).

Start with a simple object: a shoe, an apple, a leaf. Draw the outline first. Then add shading. Don’t rush.

Topic 2

Perspective

Making things look 3D on flat paper.

A road disappearing into the distance looks smaller and smaller. Trees far away are tiny compared to nearby ones. This is perspective.

Simple trick: find the vanishing point — a dot on the horizon where parallel lines seem to meet. Roads, railway tracks, and the edges of rooms all point towards a vanishing point.

Things closer to you are bigger, brighter, more detailed. Things far away are smaller, paler, less detailed. Using this in a picture makes it look 3D.

Topic 3

Colour Theory

Warm, cool, complementary.

Colours have moods:

  • Warm colours (red, orange, yellow) feel cosy, lively, hot.
  • Cool colours (blue, green, purple) feel calm, quiet, cold.

Complementary colours are pairs that sit opposite each other on the colour wheel. They make each other pop:

  • Red and green
  • Blue and orange
  • Yellow and purple

Vincent van Gogh used blue and orange together in lots of his paintings. Try it in one of your own.

Topic 4

Sculpture

Clay techniques.

Year 4 builds clay skills:

  • Pinch pot — press your thumb into a ball of clay and pinch the walls up.
  • Coil pot — roll long snakes of clay, coil them up, smooth the joints.
  • Slab — roll clay flat and cut out shapes.

When two bits of clay join, score them (scratch) and slip them (wet them) first — otherwise they’ll fall apart when dry.

Topic 5

Famous Artists

Landscape painters and portrait-makers.

Year 4 artists:

  • J.M.W. Turner (1775 – 1851) — British. Painter of stormy seas and glowing sunsets. The greatest English painter of landscapes.
  • John Constable (1776 – 1837) — British. Painter of calm English countryside scenes like The Hay Wain.
  • Vincent van Gogh (1853 – 1890) — back again, but in depth: The Starry Night, Sunflowers, the swirling brushwork.
