Topic 1

Our School & Area

Finding our way around where we live.

Your school is one of the most important places in your little world. It’s where you spend most of your weekdays, where your teachers work, and where your friends are. But have you ever stopped to think about where your school actually is?

Every school sits on a street. The street has a name. Streets join up to form a village, town or city. Your village or town is in a county. The county is in a country. The country is in the UK. The UK is part of a continent called Europe. Earth has seven continents. And so on. Every place fits inside a bigger place.

Near your school, you can probably find most of these things: shops, a park or playground, a library, a post office, houses where people live, maybe a church. All these things together make up a community — the group of people who live nearby.

If you wanted to describe where your school is to someone who’s never been, you’d need a map. A map is a picture of a place seen from above, as if you’re looking down from an aeroplane. That’s the most important tool in geography.

Topic 2

The Four Countries

The UK is four countries in one.

Did you know the United Kingdom is actually made up of four different countries, all joined together? Each one has its own capital city, its own flag, and its own traditions.

  • England (capital city: London) — the biggest of the four. It’s where most British people live.
  • Scotland (capital city: Edinburgh) — the far north. Famous for mountains, lochs (lakes), and bagpipes.
  • Wales (capital city: Cardiff) — to the west of England. Has its own language alongside English. Famous for rugby and red dragons on the flag.
  • Northern Ireland (capital city: Belfast) — the small corner of the UK that’s on the island of Ireland, across the sea.

All around the UK is the sea. That’s why Britain is called an island — actually a whole bunch of islands! The biggest island is Great Britain, which holds England, Scotland and Wales. The next biggest is Ireland, split between Northern Ireland (part of the UK) and the Republic of Ireland (a different country).

Being surrounded by sea has shaped who we are. The sea protects us, feeds us fish, and has let us sail all over the world for hundreds of years.

Topic 3

Hot and Cold Places

Some places are hot. Some places are freezing.

The Earth isn’t the same temperature everywhere. Some parts are so hot you could fry an egg on a rock. Other parts are so cold that the sea itself turns to ice. Where a place sits on Earth decides how hot or cold it is.

Near the middle of the Earth, where the Sun shines straight down, it’s always very hot. We call these parts the tropics. Animals that like heat live there: camels in the desert, lions on the grasslands, crocodiles in the swamps, elephants in the woodlands.

At the top and bottom of the Earth are the North Pole and the South Pole. The Sun’s rays hit these places at an angle, so it’s always very cold. Ice and snow cover these places all year round. Polar bears live at the North Pole. Penguins live in the south, near Antarctica.

The UK sits in between. We’re not near the Equator and we’re not at the Poles. That’s why we get four different seasons — summer can be lovely and warm, winter can be freezing, but neither one is as extreme as in the tropics or the poles.

Topic 4

Weather

What is the sky doing today?

Every single day the sky looks different. Some mornings it’s bright and clear. Some mornings it’s grey and drippy. What the sky and the air are doing RIGHT NOW is called the weather.

The UK is famous around the world for having weather that changes all the time. We can get all four seasons in a single day! Here are the main weather words you’ll need:

  • Sunny ☀ — the Sun is bright, the sky is clear or nearly clear.
  • Cloudy ☁ — the sky is covered in grey clouds. It might rain, or it might not.
  • Rainy 🌧 — water drops falling from the clouds.
  • Snowy ❄ — frozen water falling as white fluffy flakes. Everything turns white.
  • Windy 🌬 — the air is moving fast. Leaves blow around. You hold your hat on.
  • Foggy — a thick damp cloud has sat down at ground level. You can hardly see your hand.
  • Stormy ⛈ — lots of rain, wind, and sometimes thunder and lightning.

Every day, people on the TV and the radio talk about what the weather will probably do tomorrow. That’s called a weather forecast. Farmers, pilots and fishermen especially need to know.

Topic 5

Things We See in the World

Rivers, mountains, beaches.

When you look at a map of the world, you can see that there are two main types of things on Earth: things that nature made (like mountains and rivers), and things that people made (like cities and roads).

The natural features of the Earth are called physical features. These are the ones to know:

  • Mountain — a very tall piece of land, sometimes with snow on the top even in summer.
  • Hill — smaller than a mountain. Gentler to climb.
  • Beach — sand or stones right next to the sea.
  • Cliff — a steep drop at the edge of the land.
  • River — fresh water flowing all the way from the hills to the sea.
  • Lake — a big area of water surrounded by land.
  • Forest — a big place full of trees.
  • Sea — a huge area of salty water.

Then there are human features — the bits people built: towns, cities, houses, roads, bridges, farms, schools, factories. The landscape all around you is usually a mix of both.
