Topic 1

World War II (1939 – 1945)

The biggest war in human history.

World War II lasted from 1939 to 1945. It began when Germany (led by Adolf Hitler) invaded Poland. Britain and France declared war.

Two big sides:

  • Allies: UK, France, USSR, USA, China, and many others.
  • Axis: Germany, Italy, Japan.

Key events:

  • 1940 — Battle of Britain (RAF fought off the Luftwaffe).
  • 1940 — Winston Churchill becomes British Prime Minister. Famous speeches.
  • 1941 — Japan attacks Pearl Harbour; USA joins.
  • 1944 — D-Day landings in Normandy.
  • 1945 — Germany surrenders (VE Day, 8 May). Japan surrenders (VJ Day, 15 August).

At home: blackouts (no lights at night), rationing (small amounts of food per person), evacuation (children sent from cities to the countryside to stay safe).

Topic 2

The Tudors (1485 – 1603)

Henry VIII’s six wives and Elizabeth I’s Golden Age.

The Tudors were kings and queens of England from 1485 (when Henry VII won the Battle of Bosworth) to 1603.

The most famous Tudors:

  • Henry VIII — had six wives. Divorced, beheaded, died, divorced, beheaded, survived.
  • Elizabeth I — Henry’s daughter. Reigned 45 years. Her time is called the Elizabethan Golden Age: Shakespeare wrote plays, Francis Drake sailed around the world, the Spanish Armada was defeated (1588).

Henry VIII broke with the Catholic Pope and created the Church of England in 1534, so he could divorce his first wife.

Topic 3

Local History

The story of your own town.

A local history study looks at how ONE place has changed over time. You can learn from:

  • Old maps — compare 1900 vs today.
  • Census records — who lived here? What did they do?
  • Photographs — how did streets and shops change?
  • Oldest building — often a church or pub.
  • Place names — tell you who settled there (-by = Viking, -chester = Roman fort, -ham = Anglo-Saxon village).

Talking to grandparents and great-grandparents is a brilliant source (“oral history”).

Topic 4

A Theme Through British History

E.g. how crime and punishment changed.

Year 6 picks one theme and traces it through time. A popular one: crime and punishment.

  • Roman Britain: laws written down; hard punishments like crucifixion.
  • Anglo-Saxon: “wergild” — a price paid for injury.
  • Medieval: trial by ordeal (put hand in boiling water, see if it heals!). Stocks and pillories.
  • Tudor: very harsh — hanging, burning.
  • Victorian: prisons built. Transportation to Australia.
  • Today: prisons, fines, community service. No death penalty (since 1965).
Topic 5

Grand Timeline of Britain

Putting it all in order.

  • 43 AD — Roman invasion.
  • ~410 AD — Romans leave.
  • 450–600 AD — Anglo-Saxon settlement.
  • 793–878 AD — Viking raids and Danelaw.
  • 1066 — Normans invade.
  • 1485–1603 — Tudors.
  • 1603–1714 — Stuarts (incl. English Civil War, Great Fire of London 1666).
  • 1714–1837 — Georgians.
  • 1837–1901 — Victorian era (Industrial Revolution).
  • 1914–1918 — World War I.
  • 1939–1945 — World War II.
  • 1952–2022 — Elizabeth II’s reign.
