Topic 1

Writing Programs in Scratch

Making games, stories and animations.

Year 3 is where coding really opens up. We move from ScratchJr to Scratch, a free programming language used by millions of kids around the world. In Scratch, you drag colourful blocks of code that snap together like jigsaw pieces.

A Scratch program has sprites (characters or objects) on a stage (the white background). You give each sprite instructions: when the green flag is clicked, move 10 steps, play a sound, say hello.

Scratch projects can be: a game, an animation, a story, a quiz, a simulation. The same tool, thousands of different creations.

Topic 2

Loops & Repetition

Doing something again and again.

Imagine you want to draw a square. A square has four sides. You could write:

  • Go forward 100 steps
  • Turn right 90 degrees
  • Go forward 100 steps
  • Turn right 90 degrees
  • Go forward 100 steps
  • Turn right 90 degrees
  • Go forward 100 steps
  • Turn right 90 degrees

That works but it’s clunky. Instead, use a loop:

  • Repeat 4 times:
    • Go forward 100 steps
    • Turn right 90 degrees

Same result, much cleaner. Loops are one of the most useful ideas in all of programming.

Topic 3

Networks & the Internet

How computers talk to each other.

A computer network is two or more computers connected so they can share things. Your school has a network — all the computers are linked so teachers can share files and you can print to a printer down the corridor.

The internet is the world’s biggest network — billions of computers all connected to each other. When you visit a website, your computer sends a message to another computer far away (a server), which sends the page back.

Computers on the internet find each other using addresses — like postal addresses, but for computers.

Topic 4

Searching the Web

Finding what you need.

The internet has so much information that without a search engine, you’d never find anything. Google is the most famous one. Type in a word, and it gives you a list of pages that match.

Good searching tips:

  • Use key words, not whole sentences. Search “Year 3 rocks types” rather than “what are the three types of rocks in Year 3 science?”
  • Check WHICH website you’re on. BBC Bitesize is reliable. A random blog might not be.
  • If the answer looks strange, check a second source.
Topic 5

Staying Safe Online

Bigger rules for bigger users.

In Year 3 we use the internet much more. Time to level up your safety knowledge:

  • Never meet up with someone you only know online. Not everyone tells the truth about who they are.
  • Strong passwords. Never use your name or "password". A mix of letters, numbers, and symbols is best.
  • Cyberbullying is when someone is mean online. If it happens to you, save the evidence and tell a trusted adult.
  • Private things stay private. Don’t share photos of yourself or your family with strangers.
