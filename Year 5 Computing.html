Topic 1

Building Bigger Programs

Putting sequence, selection, repetition and variables together.

By Year 5 you can combine all the ideas you’ve learned — sequence (one thing after another), selection (if…then), repetition (loops), and variables — into programs that do real things.

For example, a simple quiz game uses all four:

  • Sequence: Ask a question. Wait for answer. Respond.
  • Selection: IF the answer is correct THEN add to the score.
  • Repetition: Do this for ten questions using a loop.
  • Variables: Keep a running score variable.

You can use Scratch, or try a text-based language like Python. Real programmers write in text. The ideas are the same.

Topic 2

Decomposition

Break the big problem into small ones.

Decomposition is the technique of splitting a big problem into smaller, simpler pieces. It’s how all good programmers tackle anything tricky.

Want to build a space invaders game? Don’t try to do it all at once. Break it down:

  1. Get a spaceship sprite that moves left and right with the arrow keys.
  2. Make it fire a bullet when you press space.
  3. Add enemy aliens that move down the screen.
  4. Detect when the bullet hits an alien.
  5. Add a score that goes up for each alien destroyed.
  6. Add a game-over screen when the aliens reach the bottom.

Each piece is much easier than the whole game. Finish them one at a time, and before you know it the full game exists.

Topic 3

Searching the Web Smartly

Becoming a search ninja.

By Year 5, you should be a proper internet researcher. A few tricks make a big difference:

  • Quote marks force exact matches. Search “Battle of Hastings” (with quotes) and you get pages that contain that exact phrase.
  • Minus sign excludes words. “jaguar -car” searches for jaguars but not cars.
  • Site search: “Vikings site:bbc.co.uk” only gets results from BBC.

Also learn to check RELIABILITY:

  • Who wrote the page? A museum, a university, a known organisation — good. A random blog — less reliable.
  • When was it written? Recent is often better for facts.
  • Do other sources agree?
Topic 4

Digital Media

Photos, sound, video.

Computers store every image, sound and video as numbers. A photo is a grid of tiny coloured squares called pixels. More pixels = a sharper image, but a bigger file.

You can edit digital media with software:

  • Photos: crop, recolour, rotate, add text.
  • Sound: record, cut out silence, change volume, layer tracks.
  • Video: trim clips, add music, add titles, add transitions.

You can also COMBINE them to make multimedia: a slideshow with music, a video with titles, a podcast with sound effects.

Topic 5

Staying Safe Online (Y5)

Older, more aware, more responsible.

Year 5 safety focuses on the big picture. You’re old enough now to understand the hows and whys:

  • Social media. Most platforms have a 13+ age limit. For good reasons — they can expose children to content that isn’t safe.
  • Screen time. Too much scrolling can affect sleep, eyes, mood. Balance it with outdoor time and real conversations.
  • Recognising grooming. If an adult you meet online is being TOO friendly, asking to keep things secret, or pushing you to send photos — stop and tell a trusted adult IMMEDIATELY.
  • Permanent footprint. Don’t post anything you wouldn’t want your future boss to see.
