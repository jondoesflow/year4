Topic 1

Research & Design

Looking at existing products.

Year 3 designers start with research. Before designing a new product, look at products that already exist. What works? What doesn’t? What could you improve?

This is called studying existing products. Take a torch apart (carefully!). See how the parts fit together. Notice what’s clever and what’s clumsy. All real designers do this.

Then you sketch your ideas. Label your sketches with the materials you’d use. Try three or four different designs before picking one.

Topic 2

Tools & Accuracy

Measuring and cutting precisely.

By Year 3 you use proper tools:

  • Ruler to measure and draw straight lines.
  • Pencil to mark where to cut.
  • Strong scissors for card.
  • Craft knives — only with an adult, on a cutting mat.
  • Drill (hand drill) — for making small holes.
  • Glue gun — with supervision. Hot glue sticks everything but is burn-dangerous.

Famous rule: measure twice, cut once. Once you’ve cut, you can’t un-cut.

Topic 3

Stronger Structures

Making things stand up.

Year 3 structures get more sophisticated. Techniques:

  • Triangulation — adding triangles to stiffen a frame. Bridges are full of triangles.
  • Corrugation — folding paper or card into ridges. Corrugated cardboard is stronger than flat cardboard.
  • Lamination — sticking layers together. Plywood is laminated.
  • Reinforcing corners — a weak point on any box.
Topic 4

Food

Where it comes from, and how to cook.

Year 3 looks more closely at where food comes from. Seasonality matters — fruit and veg taste best when they’re in season near where you live. UK strawberries in June. UK apples in autumn.

Food miles: how far did the food travel? Flying grapes from Chile adds carbon emissions.

Cooking skills this year: measuring with scales, stirring over low heat, using an oven with a grown-up, making simple sauces. Try: tomato pasta sauce, fruit crumble, scones.

Topic 5

Evaluating

What the world’s best designers do.

Real designers never think their work is perfect. They always evaluate and iterate (try again with changes).

Apple’s first iPhone was great. They’ve now made 15 more, each a little better. That’s iteration.

Questions Year 3 evaluators ask:

  • Did my product do its job?
  • Was it strong enough?
  • Does it look good?
  • What’s the weakest part?
  • What would I change?
