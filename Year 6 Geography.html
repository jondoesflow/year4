Topic 1

The World’s Countries & Continents

The whole map.

There are around 195 countries in the world, across 7 continents.

Biggest countries by area:

  1. Russia — by far the biggest.
  2. Canada
  3. USA
  4. China
  5. Brazil

Biggest cities by population (approximate): Tokyo, Delhi, Shanghai, São Paulo, Mexico City, Cairo, Mumbai, Beijing.

Topic 2

Latitude & Longitude

The grid that covers the Earth.

Imagine a grid around the Earth:

  • Latitude — lines that go EAST-WEST (horizontal). Measured in degrees NORTH or SOUTH of the Equator (0°).
  • Longitude — lines that go NORTH-SOUTH (vertical). Measured in degrees EAST or WEST of the Prime Meridian (0°), which passes through Greenwich, London.

Every place on Earth has a coordinate: e.g. London is roughly 51° N, 0° W. New York is 41° N, 74° W.

Special lines of latitude: Tropic of Cancer (23.5° N), Tropic of Capricorn (23.5° S), Arctic Circle, Antarctic Circle.

Topic 3

Time Zones

Why lunch in London is bedtime in Sydney.

Because the Earth spins, different parts face the Sun at different times. Noon happens at different places at different moments.

The world is split into 24 time zones, one for each hour of the day.

Rough differences from London:

  • New York: 5 hours behind London.
  • Los Angeles: 8 hours behind.
  • Dubai: 4 hours ahead.
  • Sydney: 10 hours ahead.
  • Tokyo: 9 hours ahead.
Topic 4

Economic Activity

How countries make money.

The jobs people do can be grouped into sectors:

  • Primary — getting raw materials from the Earth: farming, fishing, mining, logging.
  • Secondary — making things in factories: cars, clothes, computers.
  • Tertiary — services: teaching, nursing, shops, restaurants, tourism.
  • Quaternary — knowledge work: scientists, software engineers, researchers.

Rich countries tend to have MORE quaternary and tertiary jobs. Developing countries often have MORE primary and secondary jobs.

Topic 5

Fieldwork Skills

Being a real geographer.

Fieldwork is when geographers leave the classroom and measure real places.

Common fieldwork tools and skills:

  • Compass — to find direction (N, S, E, W).
  • OS map (Ordnance Survey) — Britain’s detailed maps with grid references.
  • Grid references — 4-figure for a 1 km square, 6-figure for a 100 m square. Read "along the corridor, up the stairs".
  • Surveys — counting traffic, asking shoppers questions.
  • Sketch maps & labelled photos — to record what you see.
