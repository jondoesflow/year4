Topic 1

The Stone Age

Life before metal.

The Stone Age is the very long period when people used tools made of stone, wood and bone. It lasted from about 2.5 million years ago to 4,500 years ago.

Three parts:

  • Palaeolithic (old Stone Age) — hunter-gatherers, cave paintings, fire.
  • Mesolithic — smaller tools, living in small camps.
  • Neolithic (new Stone Age) — first farms, first villages, pottery, Stonehenge.

Stonehenge in Wiltshire was built about 5,000 years ago in the Neolithic.

Topic 2

The Bronze Age

People learn to make metal.

Around 2,500 BC, people discovered how to make bronze by mixing copper and tin. Bronze is harder than stone and keeps a sharp edge.

The Bronze Age lasted from about 2500 BC to 800 BC. Bronze Age people built round houses with thatched roofs, and buried their important dead with valuables in burial mounds called barrows.

Topic 3

The Iron Age

Iron tools, hillforts and tribes.

From about 800 BC to 43 AD (when the Romans arrived), people in Britain used iron. Iron is even harder than bronze.

Iron Age people:

  • Lived in tribes, each ruled by a chief.
  • Built hillforts with big earth walls for defence.
  • Farmed the land and raised animals.
  • Made beautiful metal jewellery.

Druids were the Iron Age priests and wise people.

Topic 4

Ancient Egypt

Pharaohs, pyramids and the River Nile.

Ancient Egypt was a civilisation along the River Nile in Africa. It lasted for more than 3,000 years, from about 3100 BC.

Egypt was ruled by kings called pharaohs. The most famous was probably Tutankhamun — a boy king whose tomb was found full of treasure in 1922.

Egyptians built huge stone pyramids as tombs. The Great Pyramid at Giza was built about 4,500 years ago.

They wrote with pictures called hieroglyphs.

Topic 5

A Big Timeline

Seeing it all in order.

  • 2.5 million BC — Stone Age begins.
  • 3,000 BC — Stonehenge built; Ancient Egypt begins.
  • 2,500 BC — Bronze Age starts in Britain.
  • 800 BC — Iron Age starts in Britain.
  • 43 AD — Romans invade Britain.
