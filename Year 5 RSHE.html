Topic 1

Puberty

Bodies start changing.

Between ages 9 and 14, your body starts changing into an adult body. This is called puberty. It’s normal. It happens to everyone. It happens at different times for different people — some kids start at 9, some at 14, some in between. None is wrong.

Things that happen for everyone:

  • You grow taller, sometimes very fast.
  • You sweat more (so daily showers and deodorant matter).
  • Your skin can get spots or oily.
  • Hair grows under arms and around private parts.
  • Feelings can swing more easily — happy then cross then sad. Hormones in your body cause this.

Talk to a parent, carer, or trusted adult. Everyone you know who is a grown-up has been through this.

Topic 2

How Bodies Differ

Boys, girls, and changes.

Boys’ and girls’ bodies change differently in puberty.

Girls usually:

  • Develop breasts.
  • Get hips that curve more.
  • Start having periods (a small amount of blood from the vagina, every month, lasting 3-7 days). This is normal and is the body’s way of getting ready for one day being able to have a baby (much later in life).
  • Need to start using sanitary pads or other period products.

Boys usually:

  • Voice gets deeper.
  • Shoulders get broader.
  • Hair grows on face.
  • Penis and testicles grow.

Some people don’t fit neatly into either pattern. That’s OK. Bodies are diverse. The important thing is being kind to yourself and others.

Topic 3

Periods

Nothing to be embarrassed about.

A period happens about once a month. The lining of the womb (the small pouch inside the body where one day a baby could grow) breaks down and leaves the body as blood. It usually lasts 3 to 7 days.

It’s not an injury. It doesn’t hurt to bleed (though some people get cramps in the tummy).

People manage their periods with:

  • Sanitary pads — stuck inside underwear.
  • Tampons — used inside the body (older girls usually).
  • Period underwear — absorbs blood like a pad but built in.
  • Menstrual cups — a small silicone cup, used inside, washed out and reused.

Half of all humans have periods. It’s common, healthy, and absolutely nothing to be ashamed of. Boys can learn about it too — it helps everyone.

Topic 4

New Feelings

Crushes and friendships.

In Year 5 some children start getting crushes — a special, fluttery feeling about another person. It can be on a friend, a celebrity, or a character in a book.

This is completely normal. It can also be confusing. Things to remember:

  • You don’t HAVE to have a crush. People develop these feelings at different ages.
  • The crush might be on someone of a different sex, the same sex, or anyone. All are normal.
  • It’s OK to keep crushes private.
  • Don’t tease friends about their crushes — it’s mean.
Topic 5

Online & Real-World Risks

Staying safe at 9-10.

As you get older, you do more on your own. You might walk to school, message friends, or play games online. With independence comes risk.

Smart rules:

  • Tell a grown-up where you’re going and when you’ll be back.
  • If you walk somewhere, take a known route. Avoid quiet alleys at night.
  • Online: never meet up in person with someone you only met online.
  • Don’t share photos of yourself with strangers.
  • If anyone sends a message that feels weird or asks you to keep something secret from your parents — tell a grown-up STRAIGHT AWAY.
  • You can always say no, leave, or call home. You’re never in trouble for that.
