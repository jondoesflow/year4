Topic 1

North America

The continent across the Atlantic.

North America has three big countries and more than 20 smaller ones in Central America and the Caribbean.

  • Canada — capital Ottawa. Second-biggest country in the world.
  • USA — capital Washington, D.C.
  • Mexico — capital Mexico City.

Famous features:

  • The Rocky Mountains down the west.
  • The Great Lakes between US and Canada.
  • The Mississippi River down the middle.
  • The Grand Canyon in Arizona.
Topic 2

South America

The continent of the Amazon and the Andes.

South America has 12 countries. Big ones:

  • Brazil — capital Brasília. Largest country in South America.
  • Argentina — capital Buenos Aires.
  • Peru — capital Lima.
  • Colombia — capital Bogotá.

Famous features:

  • The Andes — longest mountain range in the world.
  • The Amazon rainforest — largest rainforest on Earth.
  • The Amazon River — carries the most water of any river.
  • Machu Picchu — an Inca city high in the Andes in Peru.
Topic 3

Biomes & Climate Zones

The big regions of the natural world.

A biome is a huge region with a particular climate and the plants and animals that live there. The main ones:

  • Tropical rainforest — hot, wet, trees with huge leaves (Amazon, Congo).
  • Temperate forest — four seasons, oak and beech trees (UK, most of Europe).
  • Coniferous forest (taiga) — cold, snowy winters, pine trees (Canada, Russia).
  • Savanna (grassland) — warm, dry, grasses and scattered trees (Africa).
  • Hot desert — very dry, very hot days, cold nights (Sahara).
  • Tundra — very cold, frozen ground (northern Canada, Siberia).
  • Polar — icy all year (Arctic, Antarctica).
Topic 4

Trade & Natural Resources

What countries buy and sell to each other.

Trade is when people or countries buy and sell things to each other.

Natural resources are things that come from the Earth:

  • Renewable — will never run out: sun (solar), wind, water, trees (if replanted).
  • Non-renewable — will run out one day: coal, oil, gas, metals.

Imports and exports: an import is something a country buys from another country; an export is something it sells.

Topic 5

The Water Cycle

The same cycle, but with more detail.

Water on Earth goes round and round forever:

  1. Evaporation — Sun heats water in seas, rivers, lakes. Water turns to vapour and rises.
  2. Transpiration — plants also release water vapour from their leaves.
  3. Condensation — up high, the vapour cools and turns to tiny drops, forming clouds.
  4. Precipitation — drops grow heavy and fall as rain, sleet, hail, or snow.
  5. Run-off & groundwater — water flows across the land into rivers, or sinks through the soil.
  6. Collection — all rivers lead eventually back to the sea.
