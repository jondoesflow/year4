Topic 1

Hinduism

An ancient religion from India.

Hinduism is one of the world’s oldest religions, more than 4,000 years old. It began in India.

Unlike Christianity, Judaism and Islam, Hindus believe in ONE God (called Brahman) who is shown in MANY forms. Each form is another way of understanding the one God.

  • Brahma — the creator.
  • Vishnu — the preserver, who keeps the universe going.
  • Shiva — the destroyer, who ends things so new things can begin.
  • Ganesha — the elephant-headed god of new beginnings.
  • Lakshmi — goddess of good luck and wealth.

Hindus worship in a mandir (temple) or at a small shrine at home. Their holy books include the Vedas and the Bhagavad Gita.

Topic 2

Diwali

The festival of lights.

Diwali is celebrated by Hindus, Sikhs and Jains around October or November. It’s called the Festival of Lights because people light thousands of tiny clay lamps called diyas.

Diwali remembers the story of Rama and Sita. Prince Rama’s wife Sita was kidnapped by the evil demon Ravana. Rama rescued her with help from Hanuman the monkey king. When Rama and Sita returned home, villagers lit lamps to guide them back.

Today people celebrate by:

  • Lighting diyas all round the house.
  • Drawing beautiful patterns called rangoli on the doorstep.
  • Wearing new clothes and exchanging sweets.
  • Fireworks — loads of them!
Topic 3

Easter

The most important Christian festival.

In Year 3 we look closely at Easter. Christians believe Jesus died on a cross (called Good Friday) and came back to life three days later (called Easter Sunday).

The week before Easter is called Holy Week. Key days:

  • Palm Sunday — Jesus rides into Jerusalem on a donkey. Crowds wave palm leaves.
  • Maundy Thursday — Jesus has his last meal with his 12 friends (the Last Supper).
  • Good Friday — Jesus is killed on a cross.
  • Easter Sunday — Jesus comes back to life. Christians celebrate with hot cross buns, chocolate eggs (symbolising new life) and church services.
Topic 4

Right and Wrong

What religions say.

All religions try to help people work out what’s right and what’s wrong. The teachings often look similar:

  • Don’t lie.
  • Don’t steal.
  • Don’t be cruel.
  • Be kind to parents and family.
  • Look after poor or sick people.
  • Don’t waste or be greedy.

Christians point to the Ten Commandments — ten rules given to Moses in the Bible. Muslims follow the five Pillars of Islam. Buddhists follow the Five Precepts.

Different lists. Mostly the same good ideas.

Topic 5

Sacred Places

Special buildings.

Religions have special buildings where people come together:

  • Church (Christian) — usually with a cross on top, a bell, stained glass windows.
  • Mosque (Muslim) — often with a dome and tall towers called minarets. People take shoes off before going in.
  • Synagogue (Jewish) — with a cupboard called the Ark that holds the Torah scrolls.
  • Mandir (Hindu) — colourful, often with statues of different gods.
  • Gurdwara (Sikh) — anyone can visit and eat a free meal called langar.
  • Buddhist temple — usually has a statue of the Buddha and smells of incense.
