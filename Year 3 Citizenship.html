Topic 1

What is Democracy?

Government by the people.

Democracy is when everyone gets a say in how things are run. The word comes from ancient Greek: demos (people) + kratos (power) = power of the people.

In a democracy, big decisions get made by voting. Whichever idea (or person) gets the MOST votes wins. The losers go along with the result — even if they’d have voted differently.

The UK is a democracy. So is the USA, France, India, Australia, and most of Europe. Some countries are NOT democracies — the leader gets to decide everything alone.

Topic 2

Voting

How decisions get made.

In the UK you can vote when you turn 18. Voting is a SECRET — you mark your choice on a piece of paper, fold it, and put it in a box. No one knows what you wrote.

You can practise voting in school. School councils get voted in. Some classes vote on what game to play, what book to read, where to go on a trip.

Tips for fair voting:

  • Everyone votes once.
  • Vote for what YOU think — not what your friend says to vote for.
  • Count carefully.
  • Accept the result, even if you’re sad about it.
Topic 3

Leaders

Who runs things?

Lots of people lead lots of different things:

  • Your family is led by the grown-ups in your house.
  • Your class is led by your teacher.
  • Your school is led by the headteacher.
  • Your town is led by the local council — grown-ups voted in by the people who live there.
  • The UK is led by the Prime Minister and Parliament.
  • The UK also has a King (or Queen). The King today is King Charles III.
Topic 4

Rights & Responsibilities

Two sides of the same coin.

A right is something you should always be allowed to have. A responsibility is something you should do.

Examples of children’s rights:

  • The right to an education.
  • The right to be safe from harm.
  • The right to enough food and a home.
  • The right to play.
  • The right to have a name and an identity.

With rights come responsibilities. You have the right to play — you have the responsibility to let others play too. You have the right to learn — you have the responsibility not to stop others learning.

Topic 5

Saving Money

Why and how.

You can do two things with money: spend it now, or save it for later. Both are useful. The trick is balance.

Why save?

  • So you can buy something bigger later (a bike, a games console).
  • So you can give to people who need help.
  • So you have money for surprises (like if you break your phone).

Even saving 50p a week is something. Banks help adults save — they look after the money safely and pay a little extra called interest.
