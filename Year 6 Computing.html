Topic 1

Programming in Year 6

Stepping towards text-based code.

Year 6 is where many schools introduce Python or another text-based language. It’s like Scratch without the blocks — you type the instructions.

A simple Python program:

name = input("What is your name?")
print("Hello, " + name + "!")

This asks for a name and says hello. Two lines. Short but complete.

Text-based code looks scarier but it’s mostly just typing instructions. The ideas (sequence, selection, repetition, variables) are exactly what you’ve been using in Scratch all along.

Topic 2

Inside a Computer

CPU, memory, storage.

A computer has four main parts:

  • CPU (Central Processing Unit) — the “brain”. Does calculations billions of times a second.
  • RAM (Random Access Memory) — fast, short-term memory. Holds the programs you’re using right now. Wiped when you turn the computer off.
  • Storage (hard drive or SSD) — slower, permanent. Keeps files, photos, apps when the power is off.
  • Input/output devices — keyboard, mouse, screen, speakers.

Data inside a computer is stored as binary — just two digits, 0 and 1. Every letter, picture, song is ultimately a long string of 0s and 1s. Computers love binary because it’s easy to represent with “off” and “on” electrical signals.

Topic 3

Networks & Security

How data travels safely.

You now know the internet is millions of connected computers swapping packets. In Year 6 we add security: how we stop other people reading our data.

Encryption scrambles data so only the intended receiver can read it. That’s why secure websites start with https:// (the S stands for “secure”). The padlock icon in your browser means your data is encrypted.

Threats to watch for:

  • Phishing — fake emails trying to trick you into giving up passwords or clicking bad links.
  • Malware — software designed to harm your computer.
  • Weak passwords — the biggest cause of account hacks. Use long, unique passwords or a password manager.
Topic 4

Evaluating Sources

Being a critical reader.

The internet has no editor. Anyone can publish anything. A Year 6 researcher knows how to tell good information from bad.

The CRAAP test — an easy framework:

  • Currency — when was it written? Is it still up to date?
  • Relevance — does it actually answer your question?
  • Authority — who wrote it? Are they qualified?
  • Accuracy — can you check the facts elsewhere?
  • Purpose — why was it written? To inform, persuade, or sell?

Always use more than one source. If multiple reliable sites agree, it’s probably true. If they disagree, dig deeper.

Topic 5

Online Safety (Y6)

Ready for secondary school.

Year 6 is the final year before secondary school. By now you should understand:

  • Your rights — to privacy, to feel safe, to be treated kindly, to have your reports taken seriously.
  • Your responsibilities — to be kind, not to share others’ private things, to speak up if you see bullying.
  • Managing your feelings — social media can make people feel bad. Knowing when to put the phone down is a skill.
  • Fake profiles — people online may not be who they say they are.
  • Where to get help — Childline (0800 1111), CEOP for online concerns, a trusted adult, or your school.
