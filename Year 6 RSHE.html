Topic 1

Reproduction

Where babies come from.

By Year 6, the curriculum covers where babies come from in age-appropriate, factual terms.

To make a baby, you need a tiny egg cell from a woman’s body and a tiny sperm cell from a man’s body. When a sperm cell joins with an egg cell, it can develop into a baby.

The baby grows inside a part of the woman’s body called the womb (or uterus) for about 9 months. The baby gets food and oxygen through a tube called the umbilical cord. When ready, the baby is born — either through the vagina or by an operation called a caesarean.

Some families have babies through adoption (taking on a baby born to other parents) or with help from doctors (for example IVF). All these ways of having a baby make a real family.

Topic 2

Different Relationships

All kinds of love.

People can love and live together in lots of ways. There isn’t one “right” way.

  • A woman and a man.
  • Two women, or two men.
  • Married, or unmarried but together for life.
  • Some people choose not to be in a romantic relationship at all — also fine.

What ALL healthy relationships have:

  • Respect — both people listen and value each other.
  • Trust — you can rely on each other.
  • Equality — neither one is the boss of the other.
  • Kindness.
  • Communication — you can talk about hard things.

If a relationship has shouting, hitting, controlling, or putting the other person down — that’s NOT a healthy relationship. People in those situations can get help by talking to a trusted adult or charities like Refuge or Childline.

Topic 3

Peer Pressure

Standing your ground.

Peer pressure is when other kids your age push you to do something you wouldn’t normally do. It can be small (“wear THIS”) or huge (“smoke this,” “steal that”).

Why people give in:

  • They want to fit in.
  • They’re scared of being left out.
  • The other person is forceful.
  • They didn’t know how to say no.

Ways to say no:

  • “No thanks.” (Simple. Doesn’t need a reason.)
  • “I’m good.”
  • “My parents would freak.”
  • “That’s not really me.”
  • Walk away.

Real friends accept your no. Friends who don’t are not real friends.

Topic 4

Media & Body Image

Don’t compare to filters.

Social media (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube) shows TONS of perfect-looking bodies. The truth: almost all those photos and videos are edited:

  • Filters smooth skin, change face shape.
  • Lighting is set up perfectly.
  • Photos are taken from flattering angles.
  • Many people post their best 1 photo out of 100.
  • Influencers may use surgery, dieting, or extreme exercise.

Comparing your real body, on a normal day, to those edited images, makes anyone feel bad. Don’t.

Real bodies have wobbles, spots, scars, fat, hair, freckles, stretch marks. That’s what bodies look like. They’re yours and they’re wonderful.

Topic 5

Secondary School

You’ll be ready.

At the end of Year 6 you move to secondary school. New uniform, new friends, new building, new teachers, new rules. It’s a big change.

Things that will be different:

  • Bigger school — hundreds or thousands of pupils.
  • You move to a different teacher and classroom for each lesson.
  • You’ll get homework most days.
  • You might travel further to school — bus, train, walking.
  • You’ll have more independence and more responsibility.

Things that help:

  • Learn the route before the first day.
  • Get a planner and write everything down.
  • Pack your bag the night before.
  • Be brave — say hi to people. Lots of others are nervous too.
  • Tell a grown-up if anything’s worrying you. They’ve all done it.

You’ve done six years of primary. You can do this.
